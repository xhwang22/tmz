# Manuscript style

- Use formal, direct academic prose. Present a coherent method, not a chronology
  of experiments, debugging, engineering decisions or intermediate approaches.
- Distinguish method capability, a testable hypothesis, and an empirical finding.
  Do not turn planned improvement targets into measured effects.
- Prefer precise verbs such as “revise,” “measure,” “route,” and “compare.”
  Avoid promotional novelty and universal claims about earlier optimizers.
- “Harness” is permitted as a technical noun if needed, not as a promotional verb.
- Use ERA only for the current revision method, not as the project name.
  Preference-Guided Evaluator Evolution describes the broader research project;
  the source repository's historical slide-specific name is not its scope.
  Frame the title and figures around learning evaluation programs across
  evaluation standards, with slides as one application. Do not imply universal
  cross-domain scorers or already-demonstrated transfer. Do not expose internal
  version strings or study aliases in the main paper.
- “Human preference” means overall H0 unless additional human supervision is
  explicitly documented. A model's explanation is not a human rationale.
- Use “median artifact score, then comparison,” never “majority-vote accuracy”
  for the current protocol.
- Use domain to mean an evaluation standard, not merely an artifact modality.
- Keep caveats next to claims they qualify, without repeating the draft-status
  notice in every paragraph.
- Figures should explain the scientific structure through informative artifact
  illustrations, component details, and clear evidence flow. The user requested
  richer paper-quality illustrations and explicitly authorized GPT Image 2.
  Use reviewed high-resolution conceptual artwork with recorded prompts and
  provenance; retain editable source for exact mathematical diagrams. Generated
  miniatures must not be presented as observed cases. Empirical figures require
  real, authorized analysis data.
