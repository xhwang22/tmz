\section{Quantitative evaluation: simulated displays}
\label{sec:results}

All numerical displays in this section use \emph{simulated data}, not measured
evaluator performance or collected human judgments. They instantiate the reporting
protocol while empirical evaluation remains pending. The generator, data, and
limitations are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:simulation}. No ordering among
the simulated methods constitutes evidence for either research hypothesis.

\paragraph{Alignment under distinct standards.}
The relevant comparison is evaluator evolution within a task, not one data
source against another. Table~\ref{tab:results} separates the two construction
settings and the ID/OOD endpoints. Figure~\ref{fig:alignment}a--b retains
domain-level paired effects instead of reducing heterogeneous standards to one
average. Each effect compares P2E with the static tool-augmented seed on their
jointly completed input groups. A change in pairwise agreement also leaves open
which candidates are selected; the rank-composition display in
Figure~\ref{fig:selection} makes that distinction explicit.

\paragraph{Acquisition as a separate test.}
A preference corpus can support evolution without establishing that its sampling
policy was efficient. Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}a--b specifies the separate
comparison against random sampling at equal human budgets. The endpoint is
held-out agreement after evolution, not disagreement density in the acquired
batch. The same reliability anchor is charged to all policies. Annotation
granularity is held fixed in this comparison and varied separately in
Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}b.
