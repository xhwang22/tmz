\paragraph{C1: agreement that improves selection.}
The favorable pattern would be higher ID/OOD agreement together with better Best-of-$N$ selection and lower regret (Table~\ref{tab:main-alignment}).
Figure~\ref{fig:c1-landscape}b--c separates selection from task-level agreement; an agreement gain without better selection would limit the downstream claim.
Cross-domain gains would support adapting the procedure to different standards, not transferring one evaluator universally.

\paragraph{C2: critique that improves the output.}
More human-preferred refinements, accompanied by fewer major regressions, would indicate that the evolved evaluator provides more useful guidance (Table~\ref{tab:downstream-results}).
Changes relative to $y_0$ would distinguish genuine improvement from a less damaging revision.

\begin{samepage}
\paragraph{C3: reward that improves generation.}
A favorable outcome would combine human preference for IterEval-GRPO over Seed-GRPO with retained factuality, coverage, and validity.
Higher reward alone would be insufficient; comparison with Base must also establish whether training improves generation.
Conclusions would remain task- and policy-specific, with external checks reported separately (Table~\ref{tab:external-endpoints}).
\par
\end{samepage}

\paragraph{Continuation rather than extra search.}
Higher held-out agreement than restarting and history-only proposals at matched total cost would support depth-first allocation (Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}a).
To support the proposed mechanism, trajectories must also connect a failed implementation, a diagnosed obstacle, and a later correction within the same direction.

\paragraph{Informative preferences rather than more labels.}
A favorable sampling result would reach the same held-out agreement with fewer annotation units, or higher agreement at the same units (Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}b).
Equal-label gains that disappear at equal annotation time would not establish greater human efficiency.
