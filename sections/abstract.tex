Open-ended generation is evaluated through multiple, partly overlapping signals,
yet disagreement with human judgment rarely identifies which part of an
evaluation system should change. We present \PtwoE{}, Preference-Guided Evaluator
Evolution, a framework that connects the acquisition of human feedback to the
revision and downstream use of executable evaluation programs. \PtwoE{} first
organizes existing metrics, judges, and tools in a domain-specific evaluation
semantic space. It then distinguishes disagreements within a quality dimension,
conflicts between overall and dimensional judgments, and cross-dimensional
trade-offs to guide annotation. Overall preferences, dimensional preferences,
and localized diagnostics form an adaptation evidence table. Within this
framework, \ERA{} (Evidence-Guided Revision of Agentic Evaluators) revises
artifact representations, rubrics, evaluator modules,
tools, evidence routing, and fusion while holding the base model fixed.
Direction-level hypotheses organize connected revisions, with separate decisions
for proposal, measurement, and search allocation. The research scope spans
structured visual artifacts, image generation and understanding, text generation,
and automated research. We specify controlled studies of evaluator alignment and
selection, critic-guided refinement, and reward-guided training, together with
annotation-efficiency and component ablations. These studies test evaluation and
generation separately, without assuming a universal judge.
