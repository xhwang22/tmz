Open-ended tasks admit multiple reasonable outputs, whose quality depends on
context and trade-offs that automated evaluators do not consistently capture.
Human preference data provide a reference for alignment, but a preference alone
does not fully specify the criteria and evidence needed to judge new outputs.
We introduce \ERA{}, a method for evolving evaluation agents from human
feedback. ERA treats the agent's executable evaluation program as the object of
learning, with task-specific human standards as the target. When new feedback
is needed, we organize disagreements among existing evaluation signals by quality
dimension to select cases for structured human judgments. ERA uses feedback to
form hypotheses about evaluation failures and make coordinated changes to
evaluation criteria, evidence acquisition, and judgment procedures. The resulting
programs are assessed against separate human feedback to test whether these
changes improve agreement. Across diverse open-ended tasks, our study design
examines alignment with human preferences and the usefulness of evolved
evaluators for selecting and improving outputs. Quantitative displays are
simulated; empirical validation remains pending.
