Human preferences specify desired orderings of open-ended outputs without
identifying what an evaluator must observe or how it should interpret that
evidence. We present \PtwoE{}, Preference-Guided Evaluator Evolution, a framework
for learning executable evaluation procedures under domain-specific human
standards. Two data-construction settings reuse existing preferences or collect
new preferences for tasks without suitable evaluation data. Both address the
same question of evolving an evaluator from a task and its human feedback.
\PtwoE{} maps existing metrics, judges, and tools to quality dimensions
and organizes structured feedback around semantically distinct disagreements. This
evidence grounds hypotheses about missing observations, unused evidence, or
inappropriate criteria. Within P2E, \ERA{} evolves artifact representations,
rubrics, evaluator modules, tools, evidence routing, and fusion while holding
the base model fixed. Mechanism hypotheses guide connected changes, whereas
complete programs are the units of selection. This separation permits
evidence-guided continuation without equating an unsuccessful implementation with
a refuted hypothesis. The research scope spans structured visual artifacts,
images and 3D, text generation, and automated research. We specify matched-budget
studies of feedback acquisition and evolution, followed by independent tests of
alignment and selection, critic-guided refinement, and planned reward-guided
training. Label provenance, annotation quality, and outcomes are reported
separately by construction setting. Data construction does not establish the
value of disagreement sampling, which requires a matched-budget random-sampling
control. Quantitative displays in this draft use simulated data, not empirical
results.
