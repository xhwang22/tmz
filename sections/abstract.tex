Evaluating open-ended outputs requires task-dependent judgments across multiple aspects of quality that must be balanced in context. Existing proxy metrics and model-based evaluators remain imperfect on such judgments and do not consistently align with human preferences. Humans can often tell which of two concrete outputs is better, but it is much harder to turn that ability into a comprehensive and effective evaluation procedure.
Evaluator self-evolution offers a way to learn such procedures from human feedback, but open-ended evaluation makes the evolution direction difficult to determine. The same evaluation error can suggest several plausible improvements, while a useful direction may require multiple revisions before producing a measurable gain. An unsuccessful early attempt therefore provides limited evidence about whether the direction is ineffective or simply underdeveloped. In the default evaluator-evolution setup we tested, unsuccessful early candidates were often followed by revisions in alternative directions.
We present EvalOpt, a framework for self-evolving evaluators across open-ended tasks. EvalOpt combines disagreement-based preference mining with depth-first evaluator evolution, explicitly maintaining an improvement direction across revisions and continuing it until diagnostic results support moving on or its search budget is exhausted. Unsuccessful candidates can guide further implementation without replacing the best evaluator found so far.
We specify matched-budget comparisons of preference mining and evaluator evolution, with separate assessments of held-out preference agreement, output selection, critic-guided refinement, and reward-guided training. Empirical gains remain to be established; the current quantitative displays are illustrative.
