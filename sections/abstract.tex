Human preferences can identify better open-ended outputs without specifying an executable evaluation procedure. Learning that procedure requires choosing useful comparisons and developing corrections that may need several revisions before improving agreement.

We present EvalOpt, a framework that combines disagreement-based preference mining with depth-first evaluator evolution while keeping the base model fixed. Mining prioritizes comparisons on which existing evaluation signals disagree. Evolution maintains an improvement direction across revisions and retains execution diagnostics after candidate rejection to guide the next edit or test. Direction continuation is separate from retaining the best evaluator.

Across 19 domains spanning structured visuals, images and 3D, text generation, and automated research, EvalOpt reaches 69.5% held-out decisive-pair accuracy, improving over the Seed evaluator by 8.0 percentage points and the strongest baseline by 5.4 points under matched feedback and search budgets. On four analysis domains, EvalOpt outperforms fixed-patience continuation and history-based search by 3.9 and 4.7 points, respectively, despite a similar number of revisions per direction (3.8 versus 3.6 for fixed patience), indicating that the gain comes from diagnostic-guided continuation rather than simply searching longer. Separate sampling comparisons assess feedback efficiency, while independent output judgments test whether the evolved evaluators improve critique-guided refinement and reward-guided training.
