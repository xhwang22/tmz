\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

P2E asks what an evaluator must learn beyond a mapping from outputs to scores.
A preference specifies an ordering, but explaining it requires evidence and a
standard for interpretation. Mining locates cases where existing signals fail
to settle a judgment. ERA then tests hypotheses about what to observe and how
those observations should matter, making observation, routing, and interpretation
explicit objects of evolution.

Useful exploration need not yield a program worth retaining. Connected changes
can instantiate a hypothesis without justifying its continued pursuit. The
same distinction applies downstream: correct rankings need not produce
actionable critiques, and agreement on fixed candidates need not survive policy
optimization. C1, C2, and C3 separate these uses of an evolved evaluator.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

First, dimensional judgments require additional annotation and construct
validation beyond overall preference. Second, disagreement can reflect noise
or shared bias, requiring source-dependence audits and matched-budget acquisition
studies. Third, interacting components require controlled attribution beyond
execution traces. Finally, pointwise scoring assumes a useful within-domain
ordering, and repeated development can overfit aggregate feedback. Independent
ID/OOD and generation-level studies are needed to test evaluation gains,
transfer across standards, and training benefits.
