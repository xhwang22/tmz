\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

An ordering constrains evaluation without identifying which evidence was
missing or how it should be weighted. ERA therefore treats observation, routing,
and interpretation as objects of evolution. Structured feedback informs
mechanism hypotheses; complete-program comparison determines which hypotheses
yield an evaluator worth retaining.

Label availability changes the cost of posing this problem, not its meaning.
Neither reused nor new preferences automatically validate the other; agreement
and abstention expose uncertainty in each human endpoint. Data collection makes
evolution possible, whereas a matched random-sampling comparison tests acquisition
efficiency.

C1, C2, and C3 require separate evidence: correct rankings need not yield
actionable critiques, and fixed-pool agreement need not survive policy optimization.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

First, simulated displays support no effectiveness claim and must be replaced
with frozen empirical cohorts. Second, dimensional judgments require additional
annotation and construct validation. Third, shared signal bias and interacting
components require dependence audits and controlled attribution. Finally,
pointwise scoring assumes a useful within-domain ordering, while repeated
development can overfit aggregate feedback. Independent ID/OOD and generation-level
studies must test evaluation gains and downstream utility; transfer across
standards remains a separate question.
