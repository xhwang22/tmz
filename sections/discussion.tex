\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Program revision increases the number of possible explanations for an observed
change. A connected edit may improve agreement without establishing which
component caused the effect; targeted ablations and artifact-level evidence are
needed for attribution. Human overall preference also underdetermines the
underlying criteria. An evaluator can reproduce an ordering for the wrong reason,
and source-linked findings alone do not establish faithful reasoning.

Pointwise scores make selection and refinement possible, but assume that a
context-independent quality ordering is useful within the domain. Contextual
preferences or incompatible quality dimensions may violate this assumption.
Repeated scoring reduces sensitivity to individual calls without eliminating
model bias. Adaptive development selection can still overfit, making locked
test data necessary.

Finally, tool access, human annotation, and repeated evaluation can be costly.
The proposed comparisons must therefore report both agreement and resource use.
Cross-domain transfer, improved refinement, and robustness remain open empirical
questions. The present scope does not establish benefits for reward-guided
parameter training or unrestricted autonomous agent design.
