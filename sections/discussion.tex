\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

ERA permits continuation without lowering the recorded best development score, but continuation has an opportunity cost.
If history-only search beats independent proposals yet matches ERA at equal cost, retained diagnostics may explain gains without direction continuation.
Development-only gains would instead suggest adaptation to repeatedly inspected feedback, not better evaluation of unseen outputs.
Neither outcome supports a general advantage for depth-first allocation.

Downstream use changes which errors matter.
Selection depends on ordering the best outputs; critique requires actionable diagnoses.
Training shifts outputs toward whatever the reward favors, so C1 agreement or C2 refinement cannot substitute for C3 evaluation.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}
First, consensus can hide shared errors, requiring random checks and measured human-time comparisons.
Second, diagnostics do not verify a hypothesis; independent trajectory review must include failed and exhausted directions.
Finally, sampling efficiency and continuation benefits remain unmeasured, requiring matched-budget runs before empirical claims.
