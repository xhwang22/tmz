\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Human preferences are difficult to express as executable evaluation procedures.
We study evaluation-agent self-evolution through structured feedback and
hypothesis-guided program changes, with a fixed base model. Reused and newly
collected preferences support the same problem. The study design compares
program evolution with parameter adaptation and separates preference alignment
from usefulness in guiding generation. Simulated displays do not establish
these benefits; the question is whether an evolved approximation of human
standards remains useful on unseen outputs.
