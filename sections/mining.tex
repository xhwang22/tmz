\section{Informative Preference Mining}
\label{sec:mining}

Informative Preference Mining (IPM) lowers the sampling priority of comparisons on which existing evaluation signals consistently agree.
Repeated annotations of these comparisons may add little information about evaluation errors.
IPM selects comparisons from existing datasets or newly generated outputs, reusing compatible human preferences and collecting missing ones while retaining a random anchor.

\subsection{Comparable evaluation signals}

Outputs are grouped by their original task input and deduplicated before comparison.
For a pair $r=(x,y_a,y_b)$, signal $j$ supplies a preference sign $u_j(r)\in\{-1,0,+1,\bot\}$ from its own score difference and declared tie margin.
Zero denotes a tie and $\bot$ missing or abstaining output; raw scores from different signals are never subtracted.
Signals require executable sources with compatible inputs; provenance, repeated-run variation, and missingness are recorded separately.

We freeze a many-to-many mapping $M_{jq}$ from signal $j$ to dimension $q$ using its definition and measurement procedure before reading preference labels.
A mixed score may connect to several dimensions, but remains one shared signal.
Signals sharing a model, training labels, or underlying metric are assigned source groups so that duplicated prompts do not become independent votes.
Metrics reserved for final independent assessment remain outside mining, evaluator tools, critique, and reward.

\subsection{Sampling priority}

The ranking distinguishes three cases.
\emph{Within-dimension disagreement} occurs when signals from distinct source groups favor opposite outputs on the same criterion.
\emph{Overall--dimension disagreement} occurs when an overall signal opposes a dimensional signal.
\emph{Cross-dimensional trade-offs} occur when dimension-level preferences favor different outputs; these are not contradictory measurements of one criterion.
Dimensional preferences aggregate at most one consensus sign per source group, with internally opposed groups abstaining.
Writing their indicators as $I_{\mathrm{within},q}$, $I_{\mathrm{overall},q}$, and $I_{\mathrm{tradeoff}}$, the implemented priority is
\begin{equation}
 P(r)=2\sum_q I_{\mathrm{within},q}(r)
      +\sum_q I_{\mathrm{overall},q}(r)
      +I_{\mathrm{tradeoff}}(r).
 \label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
IPM ranks pairs by decreasing $P(r)$.
This structural score does not estimate information gain or human reliability.
In particular, $P(r)=0$ can reflect missing signals or ties rather than agreement, and agreement itself need not be correct.

The random anchor precedes ranking, allowing checks for shared blind spots even among low-priority comparisons.
Anchor annotations count toward the same human budget as targeted annotations.
Human-reliability qualification remains separate from the implemented ranking (Appendix~\ref{app:annotation}).
Only evolution data undergo IPM; final evaluation uses independently specified random or stratified sampling.
Whether IPM produces more informative preferences is tested by subsequent evaluator improvement at equal human cost, not by the number of disagreements collected.

\subsection{Human feedback for evolution}

Existing preferences can be reused when the task, readable candidate outputs, and judged quality construct match.
Derived comparisons retain their ranking, rating, or error-annotation provenance.
When matching preferences are absent, the task and annotation protocol are fixed before collecting blinded judgments on existing or freshly generated outputs.

The feedback table $Z(r,q)$ links artifact provenance, available human judgments, source signals, evaluator decisions, tool observations, and execution cost.
Both construction settings supply this interface to ERA, while retaining their label origins and annotation protocols.
Overall and dimensional preferences reveal evaluation errors for ERA to investigate, with localized diagnostics and human rationales included when available.
Missing feedback levels remain absent, and model-generated explanations are not human $H_1$ or $H_2$.
