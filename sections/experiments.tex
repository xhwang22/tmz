\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments ask whether the evaluator improves judgments and downstream outputs (C1--C3), and whether preference mining and direction continuation account for those improvements under matched resources.
Readiness determines inclusion from the 22-domain scope, with a separate evaluator per standard (Appendix~\ref{app:domains}).
Existing datasets supply candidate outputs for mining or reusable preferences; missing preferences are collected on existing or new outputs.

\subsection{Core results}
\label{sec:core}

\paragraph{C1: evaluator alignment and selection.}
Table~\ref{tab:main-alignment} compares IterEval with metric references, static judges, and alternative adaptation methods.
Controlled learned comparisons share feedback and the initial model (Appendices~\ref{app:adaptation} and~\ref{app:ablations}).
For each test input, all evaluators receive the same output group, with a default design of $N=4$.
Complete human rankings support global pairwise agreement, top-region agreement involving the human top-two outputs, Best-of-$N$ selection, and selection regret.
When only ranks are available, normalized regret is $(\operatorname{rank}(\hat y)-1)/(N-1)$ for the selected output $\hat y$.
A pair-only dataset supports pairwise evaluation, not an $N$-way selection claim.

Source-disjoint ID/OOD tests preserve the evaluation standard and sample independently of mining conflicts.
For a fixed evaluator, paired effects use 95\% intervals over original-input groups; independent searches assess optimizer variation separately.
Appendix~\ref{app:measurement} specifies coverage, worst strata, repeated scoring, and uncertainty.

\paragraph{C2: critic-guided refinement.}
For each unseen input, a frozen generator produces one fresh output $y_0$.
The seed and IterEval critics inspect that same output with matched evidence and critique formats.
The same generator refines each once under identical prompts, decoding, tools, and token budgets.
New blinded rankings compare IterEval-refined with Seed-refined and assess both against $y_0$, including major regressions.
SV4 and IG1 are priorities, with TG1 testing continuity across tasks (Table~\ref{tab:downstream-results}).

\paragraph{C3: reward-guided training.}
Base, Seed-GRPO, and IterEval-GRPO share an initial checkpoint, with LoRA and group relative policy optimization for the trained arms \citep{hu2022lora,shao2024deepseekmath}.
Rewards are frozen before training.
Rollouts are sampled online by each arm's own evolving policy and are not shared.
The arms match training inputs, policy-update resources, and reward-call budgets.
New blinded judgments and reserved external metrics assess their fresh outputs.
C3 targets IG1, SV2, and the TG4 control after reward diagnostics (Appendix~\ref{app:training}).
C3 remains a design-only study here; no parameter training or training benefit is reported.

\subsection{Ablation studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

Search comparisons hold feedback fixed and count all proposal, diagnostic, measurement, and validation costs.
The decisive contrast is direction continuation versus history-matched proposals that always edit the best program (Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}a).
The primary matching variable is total model-and-tool cost under frozen prices, not the number of attempts.
Sampling policies are compared at equal human budgets $N_H\in\{20,50,100,200\}$, measured in original-input annotation units.
The endpoint is final held-out alignment under the same evolution budget, with random anchors charged to every arm (Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}b).
The decisive sampling contrast is preference mining versus random sampling with fixed feedback detail.
Equal units test label efficiency; measured annotation time is needed to test human efficiency.
Same-pair feedback-granularity tests and compatible component replacements complete the comparisons in Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}.

\subsection{Analysis and studies}
\label{sec:analysis}

Trajectory analysis links failed implementations, diagnoses, subsequent edits, and outcomes, including exhausted directions.
Paired interventions test missing observations versus their incorrect use, while same-subset comparisons assess feedback conversion.
Appendix~\ref{app:analysis-details} separates these analyses from annotation, search, and deployment costs.
