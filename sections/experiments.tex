The evaluation separates broad evaluator assessment (C1), critic-guided refinement (C2), and reward-guided training (C3).
Mining and evolution ablations test which feedback and search decisions account for evaluator quality.

\paragraph{Tasks and assessment.}
C1 targets the 22-domain scope in Figure~\ref{fig:c1-landscape}a, using direct overall preferences or new annotation where public resources supply only dimensional judgments or references.
C2 and C3 share four representative domains: webpage generation (SV4), image captioning (IG1), summarization (TG1), and research proposals (AR1).
This roster covers all four families and is fixed by public data and generator routes, not local asset availability or observed gains (Table~\ref{tab:readiness}).
Each admitted task receives a separately adapted evaluator.
Source groups are split before Train-only mining; Val and Test remain fixed across methods.
The reference 100 Val and 400 Test pairs require sufficient independent groups.
Appendix~\ref{app:domains} records each domain's public resources, label type, access conditions, and remaining annotation or integration work.

\paragraph{Comparisons and resources.}
Comparators include fixed judges, learned signal fusion, SkillOpt \citep{yang2026skillopt}, GEPA \citep{agrawal2026gepa}, and Meta-Harness \citep{lee2026metaharness}.
External optimizers retain native search policies under common feedback and cost budgets, with development access declared separately (Table~\ref{tab:adaptation}).
GEPA edits complete programs; text-only EvalOpt matches SkillOpt's edit space.
Model versions, tool access, and scoring settings are frozen per comparison.
Search cost includes diagnostics, rejected attempts, and Val checks; feedback collection and deployment costs are reported separately.
Five searches per adaptive condition are the initial target, to be fixed after a development-only pilot.
Per-domain effects and equal-domain means use paired 95\% intervals over source groups and search runs (Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}).

\subsection{Ablation studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{Preference mining.}
Mining is tested by the final evaluator obtained from a fixed feedback budget.
Random and mined subsets use the same pool, feedback detail, seed evaluator, and evolution budget, restarting search at each label budget.
Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition} specifies label-budget and human-time comparisons, including the anchor and adjudication.
Uncertainty and cross-model disagreement are simpler sampling controls (Table~\ref{tab:mining-results}).

\paragraph{Execution evidence and search control.}
The evolution studies separate diagnostic feedback from controller decisions.
Feedback controls retain contracts and controller checks, changing only returned evidence or access to earlier records.
Controller contrasts compare the previous and proposed policies with architecture fixed, then test staging and adaptive modification scope separately.
Matched-cost searches receive terminal Test assessment of Val-selected evaluators.
Parent reset and immediate branching are additional policy interventions (Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}).
