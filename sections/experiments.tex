\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments are designed to test four questions: whether revision improves
held-out human-preference agreement; whether the resulting scores select better
artifacts from a fixed pool; whether evaluator feedback improves new artifacts;
and which editable components account for any observed effect. The final
datasets, sample sizes, model revision, and budgets must be fixed before the
confirmatory runs. Appendix~\ref{app:manifest} records these outstanding choices.

\paragraph{Domains and supervision.}
We evaluate the revision method under distinct artifact-evaluation standards,
using a domain-specific adapter and evaluator for each standard. Candidate
applications include presentations, webpages, and other structured artifacts.
Candidate domains and relevant public resources are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:domains}; inclusion is conditional on authorized artifacts and
suitable human judgments. Screenshot reconstruction and
specification-driven web generation are treated as distinct domains because
their evaluation standards differ, despite sharing an output format.
Each split is grouped by original input, with near-duplicate sources audited
before any optimization. Overall preference is kept distinct from rubric scores
and model-generated explanations.

\paragraph{Baselines and controls.}
The planned comparisons comprise the seed evaluator, a fixed multi-criterion
evaluator, prompt-only adaptation, and full program revision. The prompt-adaptation
comparison includes an established reflective optimizer such as GEPA, with a
prespecified version and edit space. Program-search comparisons expose comparable
component interfaces to isolate the effect of the revision procedure.
All matched comparisons use the same base model, training supervision,
candidate pools, and measurement protocol. Search and deployment costs are
reported separately, including tool and synthesis calls. A larger inference
budget is not attributed to revision quality.

\paragraph{Preference agreement and selection.}
The primary endpoint is strict-pair agreement on locked test inputs, reported
with coverage, predicted-tie frequency, and source-grouped uncertainty intervals.
Paired differences use the same test inputs and repeated-scoring protocol.
For best-of-$N$ selection, every evaluator ranks the same prespecified candidate
pool for each input. Human assessment of the selected outputs is the endpoint;
existing pair-only labels do not establish best-of-$N$ quality.
Neither the pool size nor evaluation cohort is changed in response to a result.

\paragraph{Feedback-guided refinement.}
For each unseen input, a fixed generator first produces one fresh initial
artifact shared by all refinement arms. The seed and evolved evaluators then
provide feedback under matched budgets to the same refinement generator.
New blinded human comparisons assess the resulting artifacts; labels attached
to the original training pairs cannot evaluate these newly generated outputs.
Generator settings, refinement rounds, presentation order, and annotation
procedures are fixed in advance.

\paragraph{Ablations and reliability.}
The principal ablations remove component families or alter synthesis while
holding the remaining protocol fixed (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).
Repeated runs of a fixed evaluator estimate stochastic variability, while
multiple independent search seeds assess search variability.
Held-out generators, source distributions, or quality regimes provide
prespecified OOD tests. These analyses distinguish a stronger proposal mechanism
from more favorable selection noise or additional computation.
