\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\input{figures/ideal_scenario/numbers}
We evaluate whether EvalOpt improves evaluator--human agreement under matched budgets (C1), whether diagnostic continuation and disagreement-based mining contribute to that improvement, and whether evolved evaluators improve critique-guided refinement (C2) and reward-guided training (C3).

\paragraph{Tasks and assessment.}
C1 covers 19 equally weighted domains in four families, with a separate evaluator per domain (Figure~\ref{fig:c1-landscape}a).
SV1 uses our human-annotated slides; Appendix~\ref{app:domains} specifies the other label sources.
Original-input groups are split before Train-only mining; Val and Test remain fixed.
The primary endpoint is decisive-pair accuracy: each output is scored independently, and a predicted tie counts as an error (Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}).

\paragraph{Comparisons and resources.}
We compare EvalOpt with Seed, learned signal fusion, SkillOpt \citep{yang2026skillopt}, GEPA \citep{agrawal2026gepa}, and Meta-Harness \citep{lee2026metaharness}.
All optimizers start from the same domain-specific Seed evaluator, informed by published rubrics and protocols (Table~\ref{tab:data-sources}).

We preserve each optimizer's native search policy while matching human-feedback and search budgets.
Search costs include diagnostics, rejected candidates, and Val checkpoints; end-to-end costs additionally include mining.
GEPA can edit complete evaluation programs, while text-only EvalOpt matches SkillOpt's editable space.
GPT-5.6 Sol executes Seed and evolved evaluators and powers all optimizers.
We run five independent searches per adaptive condition.
Mining combines rubric judgments from GPT-5.5, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, and Claude Sonnet 4.6 with applicable dimensional checks.
Appendix~\ref{app:configuration} details resources and assessment.

\subsection{Direction continuation and diagnostics}
\label{sec:ablations}
\label{sec:analysis}

We isolate diagnostic continuation from retaining history or making more revisions.
Search and mining analyses use webpage generation (SV3), image captioning (IG1), summarization (TG1), and research-idea generation (AR1), prespecified for diversity and measurement feasibility and weighted equally.

\emph{History-based search} retains diagnostics but may adopt a different correction after each revision.
\emph{Fixed-patience continuation} develops each direction for a Train-tuned number of attempts, subject to common rejection, integrity, and resource limits.
EvalOpt instead conditions continuation on accumulated diagnostics for the current correction.
All three policies share confirmation, staging, and Val-retention rules.
New directions share initialization from the globally retained evaluator, resetting the panel and scope.

Component ablations test EvalOpt's internal mechanisms (Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}a).
\emph{No retained history} removes prior-attempt diagnostics from the evolution agent, while \emph{reduced feedback} withholds case-level traces but preserves aggregate outcomes and controller-required checks.
\emph{No staging} requires confirmation before advancing the working evaluator.
\emph{Immediate branching} switches direction after the first execution-valid nonconfirming candidate.
\emph{Fixed scope} disables scope adaptation, and \emph{text-only edits} restrict revisions to SkillOpt's editable fields (Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}).

Controlled cases test repair of unrealized tool calls, integration of unused evidence, and termination of harmful corrections.
Independent reviews examine these responses in natural trajectories, including abandoned directions.

\subsection{Feedback efficiency}
\label{sec:feedback-efficiency}

We assess mining by Test accuracy at matched feedback budgets.
Random sampling, uncertainty sampling, cross-model disagreement, and our mining share the pool and feedback format, restarting evolution at each budget (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}a).
The anchor ablation compares full mining with spending all labels on ranked disagreements (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}b).
A $2\times2$ comparison crosses random/mined feedback with fixed-patience/EvalOpt search to test their complementarity (Table~\ref{tab:mining-results}).

A slide study requests only sampled dimensional judgments and overall preference.
At matched initial rating budgets, mining and random sampling are compared on adjudication cost, usable feedback, Seed errors found, and held-out accuracy after revision (Appendix~\ref{app:slide-pilot}).

\subsection{Independent downstream utility}
\label{sec:downstream-utility}

C2 and C3 use the same four domains.
C2 fixes the GPT-5.6 Sol generator; C3 updates Qwen3.5-9B with a frozen GPT-5.6 Sol reward evaluator.

For each fresh output $y_0$, C2 compares no revision with three resource-matched revision arms: no-critic \citep{madaan2023selfrefine}, Seed-guided, and EvalOpt-guided.
The no-critic arm tests revision without evaluator feedback.
Blinded judgments compare outputs and assess regressions from $y_0$.

C3 tests whether offline agreement gains translate into better policies under reward optimization.
It compares Base with policies trained using frozen Seed or EvalOpt rewards under the same within-domain recipe \citep{hu2022lora,shao2024deepseekmath}.

Blinded, rubric-guided human preference is the primary downstream endpoint, complemented by browser tests, caption SPICE, and summary ROUGE-L (Table~\ref{tab:readiness}).
Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}b reports domain results and equal-domain means; Appendix~\ref{app:downstream} details configurations, controls, and regressions.
