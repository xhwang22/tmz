\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\input{figures/ideal_scenario/numbers}

The evaluation asks whether EvalOpt improves evaluator quality, whether continuation informed by diagnostics adds value beyond history and persistence, and whether mining makes limited feedback more useful.
Downstream refinement and training assess separate uses of the resulting evaluator.

\paragraph{Tasks and assessment.}
Broad evaluator assessment (C1) targets 22 candidate domains in four families (Figure~\ref{fig:c1-landscape}a).
Four representative domains receive the intensive search and mining comparisons: webpage generation (SV4), image captioning (IG1), summarization (TG1), and research proposals (AR1).
The same roster defines critic-guided refinement (C2) and reward-guided training (C3).
Each admitted domain receives a separately adapted evaluator.
Source groups are split before Train-only mining, with fixed Val and Test cohorts shared across methods.
Task admission and annotation requirements are fixed before outcomes are examined (Appendix~\ref{app:domains}).

\paragraph{Comparisons and resources.}
Comparators include fixed judges, learned signal fusion, SkillOpt \citep{yang2026skillopt}, GEPA \citep{agrawal2026gepa}, and Meta-Harness \citep{lee2026metaharness}.
External optimizers retain native search policies under common feedback and cost budgets.
GEPA edits complete programs; text-only EvalOpt matches SkillOpt's edit space.
Optimizer comparisons share the exact selected feedback, whereas end-to-end comparisons charge both preference mining and evolution.
Search cost includes diagnostics, rejected attempts, and Val checks; human effort and deployment cost remain separate.
Model versions, tool access, scoring settings, and deployment resource caps are fixed.
The initial target is five independent searches per adaptive condition, finalized using a development-only feasibility and variance pilot.
Terminal Val-selected evaluators receive a locked Test assessment, with paired uncertainty over source groups and independent searches (Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}).

\subsection{Direction continuation and diagnostics}
\label{sec:ablations}
\label{sec:analysis}

The central comparison separates continuation based on diagnostics from simply retaining history or spending longer on a direction.
\emph{History-based search} retains diagnostics but may change correction after each revision.
\emph{Fixed-patience continuation} develops each direction for a Train-tuned number of attempts, subject to the same safety and resource limits.
EvalOpt instead conditions continuation on accumulated diagnostics.
The controls share confirmation, staging, and Val retention where applicable; direction changes still require their stated reference and panel initialization.
These are policy comparisons, not interventions on a direction identifier alone.

\subsection{Feedback efficiency}
\label{sec:feedback-efficiency}

Mining is assessed by final Test agreement at a fixed feedback and evolution budget.
Random, uncertainty, cross-model disagreement, and mined subsets use the same pool and feedback detail, restarting evolution at each label budget.
Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition} separates label efficiency from annotation time and compares mining with and without its random anchor.
Anchor labels count within the same total budget.
A random/mined $\times$ fixed-patience/EvalOpt comparison tests whether sampling and continuation contribute differently (Table~\ref{tab:mining-results}).
Annotation agreement, abstention, and adjudication costs check whether targeting disagreement also selects noisier or more expensive feedback.

\subsection{Independent downstream utility}
\label{sec:downstream-utility}

C2 compares the same fresh output $y_0$, a no-critic revision \citep{madaan2023selfrefine}, and revisions guided by Seed or EvalOpt critiques at matched generator resources.
New blinded judgments assess preference and regressions against $y_0$, rather than evaluator scores.
C3 compares Base with policies trained using frozen Seed or EvalOpt rewards under the same within-domain recipe \citep{hu2022lora,shao2024deepseekmath}.
It separately tests whether offline evaluator quality survives reward optimization.
Figure~\ref{fig:downstream} and Appendices~\ref{app:downstream}--\ref{app:training} specify both assessments; C3 follows the core search, mining, and refinement studies.
