\paragraph{Tasks and assessment.}
We prioritize webpages (SV4), detailed captions (IG1), summaries (TG1), and research ideas (AR1), separately adapting each evaluator.
The 22-domain candidate scope is not a completed benchmark (Appendix~\ref{app:domains}).
The primary test uses unseen within-domain inputs, independently of mining scores; derivatives and near-duplicate sources stay together.
Out-of-distribution tests are optional.

\paragraph{Comparisons and resources.}
Table~\ref{tab:main-alignment} includes static judges, learned signal fusion, SkillOpt \citep{yang2026skillopt}, GEPA \citep{agrawal2026gepa}, and Meta-Harness \citep{lee2026metaharness}.
Official optimizers retain their native history and search policies; GEPA receives a full-program interface.
Adaptive methods share feedback, model access, tools, and resource caps; search cost includes reflection, diagnostics, failures, and validation.
Independent searches measure variation.
Text-only IterEval controls for SkillOpt's narrower edit space (Appendix~\ref{app:adaptation}).

\paragraph{Direction continuation.}
The four controls in Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}b isolate parent retention, direction priority, and history at equal cost.
Their assigned contrasts are \ResultParentGain{}, \ResultDirectionGain{}, and \ResultHistoryGain{} points, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:search-results}); independent searches must confirm this ordering.

\paragraph{Preference mining.}
Compare mined and random feedback by held-out agreement at equal revealed-label budgets, and additionally at equal human time for new annotation.
The random/mined $\times$ history-only/IterEval scenario gives a \ResultInteraction{}-point interaction, while its time-matched comparison remains favorable after charging harder annotations (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}).
Both patterns require confirmation with actual label and time records.
