\section{EvalOpt}
\label{sec:method}

A failed revision may reflect an incompletely implemented correction rather than evidence against the correction itself.
EvalOpt separates whether to continue developing a correction from whether to commit an intermediate evaluator.
Disagreement-based preference mining selects which comparisons receive human feedback; depth-first evaluator evolution uses that feedback and accumulated execution evidence to decide what to test next.
Execution checks establish what behavior was actually realized and what intermediate evidence it produced.
Train evidence governs provisional exploration and confirmation, while sealed Val comparisons govern global retention.

\subsection{Problem setup}
\label{sec:problem}
\label{sec:measurement}
\label{sec:runtime}

Starting from a seed evaluator $E_0$, EvalOpt revises an agentic evaluator $E_\theta$ using human feedback, with the base model $M$ fixed.
Its editable configuration $\theta$ specifies prompts, tool use, component connections, and scoring rules.
For a task $x$, it evaluates each output $y$ separately, without seeing the competing output or human preference, and records intermediate observations alongside its score.
An LLM-based \emph{evolution agent} diagnoses execution records and proposes revisions; each candidate is a complete revised evaluator.
Appendix~\ref{app:synthesis} describes the reference implementation.

Within a domain $D$ sharing an evaluation standard, the objective is agreement with human preferences on unseen inputs,
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\theta}\ J_D(\theta),\qquad
 J_D(\theta)=
 \Pr_{(x,y^+,y^-)\sim\mathcal P_D,\,\xi}
 \bigl[\bar s_\theta(x,y^+)>\bar s_\theta(x,y^-)\bigr].
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Here $y^+$ is the human-preferred output, $\mathcal P_D$ is the domain distribution, and $\bar s_\theta$ is the score under a protocol frozen before search, with execution randomness $\xi$.
Original-input groups are split into Train, Val, and Test before mining.
Only Train supplies labels, cases, and traces for revision.
Val cases and traces remain sealed; the evolution agent receives only the promotion indicator and retained best score.
Val and Test cohorts remain fixed, and Test is used only after the final evaluator is frozen (Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}).

\subsection{Disagreement-based preference mining}
\label{sec:mining}

Mining prioritizes disagreements among metrics and judges, while retaining a random anchor to check for errors shared by the available signals.
The Train pool contains pairs $r=(x,y_a,y_b)$ from existing datasets or outputs generated for Train inputs.
Each signal $j$ yields $u_j(r)\in\{-1,0,+1,\bot\}$: preference for $y_b$, a tie, preference for $y_a$, or unavailable evidence.
Score orientation, tie margins, and mappings to overall quality or dimensions $q$ are fixed before human preferences are revealed.
Signals sharing a model or underlying metric form a source group.

Within each dimension, a group contributes at most one vote; internal opposition yields abstention.
The aggregate dimensional preference is the sign of the sum of group votes.
We rank pairs by
\begin{equation}
 P(r)=2\sum_q I_{\mathrm{within},q}(r)
      +\sum_q I_{\mathrm{overall},q}(r)
      +I_{\mathrm{tradeoff}}(r).
 \label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
The indicators mark opposing source groups within dimension $q$, opposition between overall and dimensional signals, and opposing aggregate preferences across dimensions, respectively.
Ties and missing signals do not count as opposition.
The weighting prioritizes disagreement on the same criterion, while counting cross-dimensional trade-offs only once per pair.

Original-input hashes select the random anchor before ranking, keeping outputs from the same input together.
We fill the remaining feedback budget in decreasing order of $P(r)$, with pair identifiers breaking ties.
Selected preferences come from compatible existing labels or blinded annotation.
Reuse requires the same task and output pair and matching evaluation criteria and annotation protocol, or an explicitly audited harmonization.
Both routes preserve label provenance and missing feedback fields; unselected labels remain hidden.
Appendix~\ref{app:annotation} details signal configuration, source grouping, and anchor selection.
Mining is assessed by downstream evaluator improvement, not disagreement yield.

\subsection{Depth-first evaluator evolution}
\label{sec:proposal}
\label{sec:search}

\paragraph{Maintaining an improvement direction.}
A direction pairs a target failure pattern with a proposed correction.
Revisions that develop or test that correction remain within the direction; adopting a different correction begins a new one.
Its diagnostic history retains observed outcomes, the evolution agent's interpretations, and unresolved questions across revisions.
Search persists at the direction level, while confirmation and retention act on complete evaluators.

A controller applies prespecified update rules to a \emph{reference evaluator} $R$, a \emph{working evaluator} $W$, and a \emph{best evaluator} $B$.
$W$ supports reversible exploration, $R$ records Train-confirmed progress within the direction, and $B$ records Val-selected progress across directions.
A new direction starts with $R=W=B$.
Revisions start from $W$.
Within a direction, EvalOpt may advance $W$ provisionally, update $R$ to $W$ after confirmation, or roll $W$ back to $R$; only Val can promote a checkpoint to $B$.

\paragraph{Revising and diagnosing.}
The evolution agent revises $W$ to produce candidate $C$, using prior attempts to identify what remains unresolved.
It identifies the failures to address and the behavior intended to change or remain unchanged.
Declared predictions about intermediate outputs are recorded before execution and evaluated as mechanism checks against the resulting traces.
These traces distinguish an intended change that failed to materialize from an intermediate output that was produced but not used in scoring.
Either can motivate further work on the same correction despite unchanged preference agreement.
Observed records remain separate from the evolution agent's interpretation and survive rejection.

\paragraph{Measuring progress.}
Parent and candidate are compared on the same cases under a fixed scoring protocol and matched execution conditions.
The local comparison set combines a fixed Train panel $\mathcal D_d$ with the revision's target and contrast cases and additional Train cases for broader coverage.
Declared contrast cases identify judgments whose ordering accuracy should not decline.
Comparing $C$ with its parent on this set measures the latest revision; comparing $C$ with $R$ on $\mathcal D_d$ measures accumulated progress within the direction.

Train measures progress through repeated preference orderings, whereas Val/Test judge the ordering after aggregating each output's repeated scores under the deployment protocol.
Let $\widehat p_i(E)$ be the frequency of correct orderings for Train case $i$, reducing to a binary outcome for one execution.
For a prespecified cohort $\mathcal C$, the paired gain is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat g_{\mathcal C}(E,F)
 =\frac{1}{|\mathcal C|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal C}
       [\widehat p_i(E)-\widehat p_i(F)].
 \label{eq:paired-gain}
\end{equation}
When the gain over $R$ on $\mathcal D_d$ is positive but does not exceed its uncertainty buffer, an otherwise eligible candidate may receive one fresh measurement.
The controller remeasures $C$, its parent, and $R$ on their original comparison sets with the same repeat count.
This fresh measurement, rather than the initial one, determines the confirmation decision.
Evidence reuse, uncertainty rules, and intermediate diagnostic measures are specified in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm}.

\paragraph{Continuing, confirming, and rolling back.}
Confirmation requires gain over $R$ supported by repeated scoring, nonnegative local mean gain, and no violation of declared contrast or mechanism checks.
Each declared contrast must avoid a decrease in mean accuracy, and every declared mechanism check must pass.
An execution-valid but unconfirmed candidate may instead remain provisional as $W$.
A \emph{scaffolding step} adds an intermediate capability for a later revision to use, and declares that intended use and an intermediate success condition.
A provisional chain shares a fixed exploration window and loss limit relative to $R$; staging another candidate does not reset the window.

With complete measurements, a supported loss or failed declared mechanism check can reject the candidate and discard the provisional chain by restoring $W$ to $R$.
Diagnostic history survives rollback, which does not by itself end the direction; only Val checkpoint selection updates $B$.
Missing required measurement outcomes instead terminate search, retaining the last valid $B$.
This execution-integrity stop reflects an incomplete comparison, not evidence that the correction is ineffective.
While the direction remains active, the next revision or diagnostic addresses the same correction.
A new direction is proposed only after the evolution agent records an ending decision based on accumulated diagnostics, consecutive rejections reach their limit, or direction resources are exhausted.
The controller enforces these limits, while the evolution agent interprets the evidence and maintains the direction's semantic continuity.

\paragraph{Adapting the modification scope.}
We use a trust-region-inspired policy~\citep{conn2000trust} to adapt the permitted scope of each revision from $W$.
The allowed changes range from local edits to structural changes within one component group and coordinated changes across groups.
Consecutive confirmed gains allow a wider scope; measured harmful changes trigger rollback and scope contraction.
Character counts and calls serve as resource bounds rather than a measure of semantic distance.
Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} gives the complete scope-update policy.

\paragraph{Global checkpoint selection.}
At prespecified checkpoints, an execution-valid $W$ is compared with $B$ on the complete frozen Val cohort.
The prespecified improvement criterion determines whether $B$ is replaced, separately from Train confirmation or direction continuation.
To limit information from repeated Val access, we withhold per-case diagnostics, following the restricted-feedback motivation of the Ladder~\citep{blum2015ladder}.
Val does not override the current direction's Train decisions.
After the final checkpoint, search returns $B$ for one locked Test assessment.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:era} gives the full loop.
