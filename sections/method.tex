\section{EvalOpt}
\label{sec:method}

A revision that fails to improve an evaluator may leave its intended correction unfinished rather than show that the correction is unhelpful.
EvalOpt therefore separates selecting an evaluator from continuing its improvement direction.
Disagreement-based preference mining selects human feedback; depth-first evaluator evolution uses that feedback and accumulated execution records to decide what to test next.
Execution checks establish what ran; Train evidence governs bounded exploration and rollback; sealed Val comparisons govern global retention.
This separation permits intermediate implementations without treating them as confirmed improvements.

\subsection{Problem setup}
\label{sec:problem}
\label{sec:measurement}
\label{sec:runtime}

Starting from a seed evaluator $E_0$, EvalOpt revises an agentic evaluator $E_\theta$ using human feedback, with the base model $M$ fixed.
Its editable configuration $\theta$ specifies prompts, tool use, component connections, and scoring rules.
For a task $x$, it evaluates each output $y$ separately, without seeing the competing output or human preference, and records intermediate observations alongside its score.
An LLM-based \emph{evolver} diagnoses execution records and proposes revisions; each candidate is a complete revised evaluator.
Appendix~\ref{app:synthesis} describes the reference implementation.

Within a domain $D$ sharing an evaluation standard, the objective is agreement with human preferences on unseen inputs,
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\theta}\ J_D(\theta),\qquad
 J_D(\theta)=
 \Pr_{(x,y^+,y^-)\sim\mathcal P_D,\,\xi}
 \bigl[\bar s_\theta(x,y^+)>\bar s_\theta(x,y^-)\bigr].
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Here $y^+$ is the human-preferred output, $\mathcal P_D$ is the domain distribution, and $\bar s_\theta$ is the score under a protocol frozen before search, with execution randomness $\xi$.
Original-input groups are split into Train, Val, and Test before mining.
Only Train supplies labels, cases, and traces for revision.
Val cases and traces remain sealed; the evolver receives only the promotion indicator and retained best score.
Val and Test cohorts remain fixed, and Test is used only after the final evaluator is frozen (Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}).

\subsection{Disagreement-based preference mining}
\label{sec:mining}

Mining prioritizes disagreements among metrics and judges, while retaining a random anchor to check for errors shared by the available signals.
The Train pool contains pairs $r=(x,y_a,y_b)$ from existing datasets or outputs generated for Train inputs.
Each signal $j$ yields $u_j(r)\in\{-1,0,+1,\bot\}$: preference for $y_b$, a tie, preference for $y_a$, or unavailable evidence.
Score orientation, tie margins, and mappings to overall quality or dimensions $q$ are fixed before human preferences are revealed.
Signals sharing a model or underlying metric form a source group.

Within each dimension, a group contributes at most one vote; internal opposition yields abstention.
The aggregate dimensional preference is the sign of the sum of group votes.
We rank pairs by
\begin{equation}
 P(r)=2\sum_q I_{\mathrm{within},q}(r)
      +\sum_q I_{\mathrm{overall},q}(r)
      +I_{\mathrm{tradeoff}}(r).
 \label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
The indicators mark opposing source groups within dimension $q$, opposition between overall and dimensional signals, and opposing aggregate preferences across dimensions, respectively.
Ties and missing signals do not count as opposition.
The fixed weights prioritize disagreement about the same criterion without treating cross-dimensional trade-offs as repeated contradictions.

Original-input hashes select the random anchor before ranking, keeping outputs from the same input together.
The remaining feedback budget follows decreasing $P(r)$, with pair identifiers breaking ties.
Selected preferences come from compatible existing labels or blinded annotation.
Reuse requires the same task and output pair and matching evaluation criteria and annotation protocol, or an explicitly audited harmonization.
Both routes preserve label provenance and missing feedback fields; unselected labels remain hidden.
Signal inventories and anchor sizes are specified in Appendix~\ref{app:annotation}.
Mining is assessed by downstream evaluator improvement, not disagreement yield.

\subsection{Depth-first evaluator evolution}
\label{sec:proposal}
\label{sec:search}

\paragraph{Maintaining an improvement direction.}
A direction identifies a target failure pattern and a proposed correction.
Its diagnostic history retains observed outcomes, the evolver's interpretations, and unresolved questions across revisions.
Refining the implementation continues the direction; replacing its correction starts another direction.
The direction is the unit of continued exploration; the complete evaluator is the unit of selection.

The controller maintains an \emph{anchor evaluator} $R$, a \emph{working evaluator} $W$, and the Val-selected \emph{best evaluator} $B$.
A new direction starts with $R=W=B$.
The anchor is the reference for Train progress, updated only when the confirmation rule is met; working is the parent of the next revision and may contain changes whose benefit remains uncertain.
Keeping these roles separate allows further implementation without discarding the last supported reference.

\paragraph{Revising and diagnosing.}
The evolver revises $W$ to produce candidate $C$, using prior attempts to identify what remains unresolved.
A revision can change a rule, introduce a boundary example, or reconnect components.
The evolver examines whether the old undesired output would still satisfy the revised rule on the same input, specifying what should change and what should remain unchanged.
When it declares an observable prediction, the prediction is recorded before execution.
Execution checks establish whether the change was installed and ran; traces then reveal whether the expected output appeared and reached its consumer.
A missing observation calls for a different next test from an observation that was produced but ignored.
Observed records remain separate from the evolver's interpretation and survive rejection.

\paragraph{Measuring progress.}
Each paired comparison between parent and candidate fixes cases, the measurement protocol, budgets, and inputs outside the intervention.
Compatible upstream observations can be reused to locate a change, but such measurements describe behavior conditional on that evidence, not independently repeated execution of the entire evaluator.
A fixed Train panel $\mathcal D_d$ provides the direction's reference cases; additional Train cases check effects beyond the panel.
The candidate is compared with its immediate parent on the local cohort and with $R$ on $\mathcal D_d$, so changing parents does not erase accumulated losses on the panel.

Let $\widehat p_i(E)$ be the observed frequency of a correct preference ordering under the frozen scoring protocol, reducing to a binary outcome for one execution.
For a prespecified cohort $\mathcal C$, the paired gain is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat g_{\mathcal C}(E,F)
 =\frac{1}{|\mathcal C|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal C}
       [\widehat p_i(E)-\widehat p_i(F)].
 \label{eq:paired-gain}
\end{equation}
Counterexample losses and execution variation are checked separately from this mean.
Margins and intermediate outputs guide diagnosis but do not replace correct preference ordering.
Positive but uncertain gains may receive one symmetric confirmation batch under a frozen rule; the new batch governs the decision even when it is less favorable.
Missing outcomes cannot confirm improvement, and a comparison that remains uncertain is recorded as unresolved.
Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} specifies the measurement scope and empirical uncertainty buffer.

\paragraph{Continuing, confirming, and rolling back.}
Train measurements determine whether $C$ becomes the anchor, remains provisional, or is discarded.
Confirmation requires repeated gain against $R$, no protected-case regression, nonnegative local mean gain, and no failed or unresolved declared mechanism check.
An undeclared check is not evidence that a mechanism succeeded.
A valid but unconfirmed candidate can become $W$ within a fixed exploration window and loss limit.
A scaffolding step additionally declares its next integration step and intermediate success condition.

A new provisional candidate does not restart its exploration window.
Rollback need not end the direction, and failure to replace $B$ does not trigger branching.
While the direction remains active, the next revision or diagnostic addresses the same correction.
The evolver records its reason for ending; rejection and resource limits also bound continuation.
The controller enforces these limits, while the evolver interprets the evidence and maintains the semantic continuity of the direction.

\paragraph{Adapting the modification scope.}
The trust-region idea~\citep{conn2000trust} limits the permitted changes around the anchor, from local edits to structural changes within a component group and coordinated changes across groups.
Consecutive supported updates allow a wider scope; harmful changes lead to rollback and a narrower scope.
Character counts and calls remain resource bounds, not a semantic metric.
This is an empirical exploration policy, not a convergence guarantee; exact rules and the optional observed-to-predicted gain ratio are given in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm}.

\paragraph{Selecting the final evaluator.}
At prespecified checkpoints, an execution-valid $W$ is compared with $B$ on the complete frozen Val cohort.
The fixed improvement criterion determines whether $B$ is replaced, separately from Train confirmation or direction continuation.
Restricted feedback follows the motivation of the Ladder~\citep{blum2015ladder}; Val does not supply per-case diagnoses or override the current direction's Train decisions.
After the final checkpoint, search returns $B$ for one locked Test assessment.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:era} gives the full loop.
