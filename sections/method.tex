\section{EvalOpt}
\label{sec:method}

A failed revision may reflect an incompletely implemented correction rather than evidence against the correction itself.
EvalOpt separates direction continuation from evaluator retention.

\subsection{Problem setup and overview}
\label{sec:problem}
\label{sec:measurement}
\label{sec:runtime}

Starting from a seed evaluator $E_0$, EvalOpt revises an agentic evaluator $E_\theta$ using human feedback, with the base model $M$ fixed.
Its editable configuration $\theta$ specifies prompts, tool use, component connections, and scoring rules.
For a task $x$, it evaluates each output $y$ separately, without seeing the competing output or human preference, and records intermediate observations alongside its score.
An LLM-based \emph{evolution agent} diagnoses execution records and proposes revisions; each candidate is a complete revised evaluator.
Appendix~\ref{app:runtime-config} specifies the shared implementation settings.

Within a domain $D$ sharing an evaluation standard, the objective is agreement with human preferences on unseen inputs,
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\theta}\ J_D(\theta),\qquad
 J_D(\theta)=
 \Pr_{(x,y^+,y^-)\sim\mathcal P_D,\,\xi}
 \bigl[\bar s_\theta(x,y^+)>\bar s_\theta(x,y^-)\bigr].
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Here $y^+$ is the human-preferred output and $\mathcal P_D$ is the domain distribution.
The deployment score $\bar s_\theta$ aggregates repeated executions by their median under a protocol frozen before search, with execution randomness $\xi$ (Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}).
Original-input groups are split into Train, Val, and Test before mining.
Only Train supplies labels, cases, and traces for revision.
Val selects the retained evaluator, and Test assesses it after search is frozen.
Both held-out cohorts remain fixed (Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}).

Disagreement-based preference mining selects which comparisons receive human feedback; depth-first evaluator evolution uses that feedback and accumulated execution evidence to decide what to test next.
Within one direction, revisions are executed and diagnosed, then confirmed, staged, or rolled back.
Candidate decisions and direction continuation are separate: an unsuccessful revision can still guide the next attempt at the same correction.

\subsection{Disagreement-based preference mining}
\label{sec:mining}

Mining prioritizes disagreements among metrics and judges, while retaining a random anchor to check for errors shared by the available signals.
The Train pool contains pairs $r=(x,y_a,y_b)$ from existing datasets or outputs generated for Train inputs.
Each signal $j$ yields $u_j(r)\in\{-1,0,+1,\bot\}$: preference for $y_b$, a tie, preference for $y_a$, or unavailable evidence.
Score orientation, tie margins, and mappings to evaluation dimensions are fixed before human preferences are revealed.
To avoid counting related signals as independent votes, signals sharing a model or underlying metric form a source group.
Within each quality dimension $q$, a group contributes at most one vote; internal opposition yields abstention.
We rank pairs by the number of dimensions with opposing source-group preferences,
\begin{equation}
 P(r)=\sum_q I_{\mathrm{within},q}(r).
 \label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
The indicator equals one when distinct source groups favor opposite outputs on the same dimension $q$.
Ties and missing signals do not count as opposition.
Preferences on different dimensions, or on overall quality versus a dimension, are not compared for conflict.

Original-input hashes select the random anchor before ranking, keeping outputs from the same input together.
We fill the remaining feedback budget in decreasing order of $P(r)$, with pair identifiers breaking ties.
Selected preferences come from compatible existing labels or blinded annotation.
Reuse requires the same task and output pair and matching evaluation criteria and annotation protocol, or an explicitly audited harmonization.
Both routes preserve label provenance and missing feedback fields; unselected labels remain hidden.
Appendix~\ref{app:annotation} details signal configuration, source grouping, and anchor selection.
Mining is assessed by downstream evaluator improvement, not disagreement yield.

\subsection{Depth-first evaluator evolution}
\label{sec:proposal}
\label{sec:search}

Depth-first evolution develops one correction across attempts, using diagnostics to assess whether an unsuccessful revision reflects incomplete implementation or evidence against the correction.
The candidate decision is \emph{confirm}, \emph{stage}, or \emph{roll back}; the direction decision is \emph{continue} or \emph{end}.
Diagnostics persist across both decisions, including candidate rejection.

\paragraph{Direction and evaluator states.}
A direction $d$ pairs a target failure pattern with a proposed correction.
Revisions that develop or test that correction remain within the direction; adopting a different correction begins a new one.
Its history $H$ retains observed outcomes, the evolution agent's interpretations, and unresolved questions across revisions.

Evaluator states separate provisional exploration, within-direction progress, and global retention.
The \emph{working evaluator} $W$ determines what to try next and may contain provisional changes.
The \emph{reference evaluator} $R$ is the within-direction comparison and rollback point, updated by locally confirmed Train progress.
The \emph{best evaluator} $B$ is the Val-selected evaluator retained across directions and returned after search.
Each new direction restarts from the globally retained evaluator, setting $R\gets B$ and $W\gets B$.
Changes from one direction carry into the next only through Val retention.
A controller enforces the state updates; the evolution agent interprets diagnostics and chooses revisions.

\paragraph{Revising and diagnosing.}
The evolution agent revises $W$ within a controller-bounded modification scope to produce candidate $C$, using prior attempts to identify what remains unresolved.
The agent identifies the failures to address and the behavior intended to change or remain unchanged.
Declared contrast cases identify judgments whose ordering accuracy should not decline.
Declared predictions about intermediate outputs are recorded before execution and evaluated as mechanism checks against the resulting traces.

Diagnosis links the intended correction to its execution, the use of resulting evidence in scoring, and the observed ordering changes.
An unrealized change motivates an implementation repair; realized but unused evidence motivates a revision to how scoring consumes it.
When the intended behavior is realized, case-level gains and regressions inform whether the correction needs refinement or reconsideration.
Observed records remain separate from the evolution agent's interpretation and survive rejection.

\paragraph{Measuring candidate progress.}
Parent and candidate are compared on the same cases under a fixed scoring protocol and matched execution conditions.
The local comparison set combines a fixed Train panel $\mathcal D_d$ with the revision's target and contrast cases and additional Train cases for broader coverage.
Local gain compares $C$ with its parent on this set, whereas accumulated gain compares $C$ with $R$ on $\mathcal D_d$.
For each case, accuracy is the fraction of repeated executions that rank the human-preferred output strictly higher, and gain averages the differences in these accuracies over the comparison cases.
A gain or loss is supported when its magnitude exceeds the empirical repeat-variability buffer; the buffer is a decision heuristic, not a significance test.
An otherwise eligible candidate with positive but uncertain gain over $R$ may receive one prespecified fresh measurement on the same cases with the same repeat count to determine its candidate decision.
Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} specifies the estimator, buffer, fresh-measurement eligibility, and execution-integrity rules.

\paragraph{Candidate and direction decisions.}
These measurements and declared checks determine the candidate-level decision.
With complete measurements, rejection checks take precedence over confirmation and staging.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lYP{0.23\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Decision & Evidence or condition & State update\\
\midrule
Confirm & Supported gain over $R$, nonnegative local gain, and declared checks satisfied & $R\gets C$, $W\gets C$\\
Stage & Execution-valid, unconfirmed candidate passing rejection checks within provisional limits & $W\gets C$; $R$ unchanged\\
Roll back & Rejection condition or provisional limit reached & $W\gets R$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

For confirmation, each declared contrast must avoid a decrease in mean accuracy, and every declared mechanism check must pass.
A contrast decline blocks confirmation but does not alone trigger rollback.
Staging requires execution validity, no rejection condition, and remaining allowance under the shared loss and step limits.
Zero or uncertain gain alone does not trigger rejection.
An edit that leaves the scoring computation unchanged is rejected unless predeclared as scaffolding and validated by a separate execution preflight.
A scaffolding step declares its downstream use and success condition; once connected to scoring, staging requires passed intermediate mechanism checks.
Successive staged candidates form a provisional chain.
The chain shares a fixed exploration window and loss limit relative to $R$, neither reset by staging another candidate.
For edits that change scoring, a supported loss relative to the parent or $R$, or a failed declared mechanism check, triggers rollback while retaining diagnostics.

The evolution agent records a continue-or-end decision and its reason based on accumulated diagnostics.
This is an agent judgment, not a deterministic test that the correction is ineffective.
While the direction remains active, the next revision or diagnostic addresses the same correction.
A direction ends on the recorded ending decision, the consecutive-rejection limit, or resource exhaustion.
Rollback alone neither ends the direction nor erases $H$; reaching a limit does not establish that the correction is ineffective.

\paragraph{Global retention.}
Val/Test evaluate the deployment protocol, which aggregates each output's repeated scores before comparing outputs.
Train's repeated ordering frequencies therefore guide search rather than directly estimating the deployment objective $J_D$.
At prespecified checkpoints, an execution-valid $W$ is compared with $B$ on the complete frozen Val cohort.
Train confirmation governs local development; Val governs which evaluator to retain across directions.
A staged $W$ is therefore eligible, but replaces $B$ only under the prespecified Val improvement criterion.
Promotion changes only $B$, not $R$, $W$, or the current direction decision.
Val cases and traces remain sealed; the evolution agent receives only the promotion indicator and retained best score.
Withholding per-case diagnostics follows the restricted-feedback motivation of the Ladder~\citep{blum2015ladder}.
This limits feedback but does not eliminate adaptive-selection risk.
After the final checkpoint, search returns $B$ for one locked Test assessment.
