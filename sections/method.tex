\section{ERA: evidence-guided evaluator evolution}
\label{sec:method}

ERA evolves an evaluation procedure by changing the program that implements it.
Its central distinction is between accepting a candidate and continuing its direction: a failed candidate may still reveal how to make the intended improvement work.

\subsection{Executing and measuring an evaluator}
\label{sec:runtime}
\label{sec:measurement}

Each invocation evaluates one artifact through the program in Equation~\ref{eq:program}.
Adapters expose permitted representations, such as source text, rendered views, or spatial structure.
Evaluator modules apply their criteria and use routing rules to obtain tool observations, then return findings with supporting evidence and uncertainty.
An available tool affects judgment only if its output reaches a module that uses it (Figure~\ref{fig:runtime}).
The default semantic synthesis groups these findings by their consequences for quality and assigns an overall score on its own anchored scale.
It receives specialist findings, reasons, and scopes, but not their structured scores, ratings, or score scales.
Numerical fusion is an explicit alternative (Appendix~\ref{app:synthesis}).

Full-evaluator development and terminal validation request $R\geq3$ executions per artifact under a fixed protocol.
Artifact scores are aggregated before comparison:
\begin{equation}
 \bar{s}_\theta(x,y)=\median_{r=1,\ldots,R}s_\theta^{(r)}(x,y),\qquad
 z_i=\ind[\bar{s}_\theta(x_i,y_i^+)>\bar{s}_\theta(x_i,y_i^-)].
 \label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
Here $y_i^+$ denotes the human-preferred artifact, which is never identified to the scorer.
Development agreement is the mean of $z_i$ over the fixed cohort, with predicted ties counted as incorrect on strict-preference pairs.
Missing executions retain their denominators and are reported through coverage and missing-outcome bounds; human ties have a separate prespecified policy (Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}).

\subsection{Turning an evaluation error into a candidate}
\label{sec:proposal}

A direction specifies an intended correction to the evaluation procedure; its hypothesis explains why that correction should improve a judgment.
For example, detecting an important omission may require locating the missing source content, assessing its importance, and making the final score reflect that assessment.

The agent investigates errors using artifacts, human feedback, and traces from a proposal subset $\Dprop$.
It can inspect component instructions and failed attempts to decide which changes are needed.
In one tool-driven session, it can request additional $\Dprop$ measurements or component probes before submitting actual edits, a short intent, and references to inspected training cases.
Expected orderings and intermediate observations can also be declared before measurement.
A sealed training subset $\Ddev$, disjoint from $\Dprop$ by original input and artifact identity, returns aggregate development measurements without exposing its cases or labels.

A candidate is a complete evaluator version implementing a direction, not an individual edit.
Edits can change any of $(A,K,V,T,\Pi,G)$ while keeping the base model fixed.
Connected changes, such as a tool, its route, and its consuming module, are assembled against one parent and evaluated as one complete candidate.
Its outcome is not credited separately to every edit.
The direction is the unit of continued exploration; the complete program is the unit of selection.

\subsection{Separating acceptance from continuation}
\label{sec:search}

Search retains a best evaluator $B$ and a working evaluator $W$ for the active direction.
A new direction starts from $B$; subsequent attempts start from an eligible $W$.
Depth-first continuation gives the active direction the next editing opportunity, using its diagnostics to address the remaining implementation problem until it is paused or search ends.
Every valid, distinct candidate receives the common development measurement, irrespective of whether local diagnostics were favorable.

\paragraph{Candidate acceptance.}
A candidate replaces $B$ only if development agreement strictly improves, execution is complete and comparable, coverage does not decrease, and the prespecified stratum-regression guard holds.
These conditions select a development best; they are not a significance test.

\paragraph{Direction continuation.}
A measured candidate can become $W$ without replacing $B$.
Besides accepted candidates, complete and comparable candidates with no observed damage remain eligible.
Damage is checked on local cases and in aggregate against both the parent and best evaluator.
Retaining an unaccepted candidate with observed damage additionally requires new measured progress and no damage to declared protection cases.
Progress means either a first, previously uncredited repair of an error present at the direction's start or a previously uncredited trace-field change matching a predeclared exact value.
Repairing damage introduced by an earlier attempt cannot repeatedly earn this credit.
If inheritance is not justified, $W$ returns to $B$, while the failed attempt remains available for diagnosis.
Thus rejecting an implementation need not erase what search learned from it or end work on its direction.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:era} and Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} detail the loop and its checks.

\subsection{Interpreting failure and ending search}

Falsification guides the interpretation of a failed attempt: did the proposed behavior occur, and did it affect the judgment as hypothesized?
In the omission example, finding missing content without using its importance in the score exposes an implementation obstacle without establishing that the direction is unhelpful.
The next attempt can address that obstacle while preserving the best evaluator.
A failed candidate does not by itself refute its direction's hypothesis.
Refutation requires diagnostic results that contradict the hypothesis within its stated scope after relevant implementation alternatives have been examined.
The current controller implements measurement, inheritance, and continuation or pausing; it does not automatically adjudicate semantic refutation.
Search is bounded by declared rounds and execution limits, and resource exhaustion leaves an unresolved direction rather than a refuted one.

On normal completion, eligible, previously accepted candidates are shortlisted and ordered using training measurements alone.
Repeated access makes $\Ddev$ adaptive development data, not an independent test.
Terminal validation $\Dval$ assesses this frozen shortlist without reranking it or feeding further edits; with no eligible improvement, search retains the seed.
Independent Test/OOD assessment follows method and analysis freeze (Appendix~\ref{app:boundaries}).
