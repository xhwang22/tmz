\subsection{Feedback and preference mining}
\label{app:annotation}
Human feedback has four levels: $H_0$ gives overall preferences; $H_1$ gives dimensional preferences; $H_2$ identifies error type, severity, and location; $H_3$ optionally supplies a free-form rationale.
Unavailable dimensions and rationales remain absent.
Existing-label replay counts revealed source groups and pair labels; new annotation also records requested ratings, person-minutes, abstentions, and adjudication.
Annotation fixes the rubric, evidence, tie policy, rater qualifications, compensation, and review requirements.
The initial design uses two independent development judgments with adjudication and three final judgments, subject to a domain-specific freeze.
Method identity, earlier winners, and model scores are hidden, and presentation order is randomized.

Mining operates only on Train, after group-level splitting and deduplication.
Select the random anchor by original-input hashes independently of scores, with its labels counted within the same total budget.
Signal signs, source groups, dimension mappings, orientations, tie margins, and repeat counts are fixed before selected human labels are revealed.
For each dimension, a source group contributes at most one sign; opposing signs within a group yield abstention.
The dimension preference is the sign of the sum of source-group votes, with zero yielding no preference.
The structural score in Equation~\ref{eq:conflict} determines rank order, with pair identifiers breaking ties.

A model signal uses the median of same-repeat score differences; this differs from comparing each artifact's median final score in Equation~\ref{eq:measurement}.
The native runner uses three repeats and a two-point tie margin for model scores; metric bundles specify their own margins.
A missing required repeat makes a signal unavailable, not an agreement.
Metrics reserved for final assessment are excluded from mining and evaluator tools.
Domain signal rosters and anchor fractions remain study-specific settings to freeze before execution.

\label{app:boundaries}
\label{app:measurement}
Original-input groups and larger declared dependence groups are assigned to Train, Val, and Test before mining.
Document derivatives, website templates, related images, and research topics remain in the same partition where applicable.
All methods share the frozen source pool, eligibility rules, splits, and held-out pairs.
Split assignment uses neither labels nor mining scores; Val/Test cohorts receive no later additions or replacements.
The deployed scorer sees one output and permitted evidence, without opponents, human labels, generator identity, or split metadata.
C2/C3 generate separate assessment outputs rather than adding to the search cohorts.

\paragraph{Accuracy and missing outcomes.}
Train uses mean correct-ordering frequencies across repeated executions for each case, as in Equation~\ref{eq:paired-gain}.
Val/Test instead uses a frozen scoring protocol with $K\geq3$ executions per artifact and median aggregation.
For strict human pairs $P_g$ within source group $g$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bar s_\theta(x,y)&=\median_{r=1,\ldots,K}s_\theta^{(r)}(x,y),\\
 v_{gj}&=\ind[\bar s_\theta(x_g,y_{gj}^{+})>\bar s_\theta(x_g,y_{gj}^{-})],\\
 \widehat J(\theta)&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal G|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}
       \frac{1}{|P_g|}\sum_{j\in P_g}v_{gj}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
Predicted ties count as errors; human ties and groups without strict preferences are counted separately.
Score-tie rates use the same strict pairs and weights as accuracy.
With fixed weights $w_{gj}=1/(|\mathcal G||P_g|)$ and completion indicators $c_{gj}$, define
$M_{\mathrm{cov}}=\sum w_{gj}c_{gj}$ and $K_{\mathrm{corr}}=\sum w_{gj}c_{gj}v_{gj}$.
Report conditional accuracy $K_{\mathrm{corr}}/M_{\mathrm{cov}}$, completion counts, and the full-cohort missing-outcome bounds
\begin{equation}
 [K_{\mathrm{corr}},\,K_{\mathrm{corr}}+1-M_{\mathrm{cov}}].
 \label{eq:missing}
\end{equation}
These are bounds, not confidence intervals.
Comparisons use a common complete cohort, with requested-cohort bounds alongside them; missing outcomes are never replaced by zeros or selectively favorable retries.
Best-of-$N$ and regret require a separate fully ranked cohort.
Native correlations, score-derived preferences, and direct human choices retain their distinct definitions.

\paragraph{Aggregation and uncertainty.}
First average within-domain accuracy over independent searches, then average domains equally.
Overall therefore weights the four family summaries by $6/20$, $6/20$, $5/20$, and $3/20$.
All methods share the endpoint's domain roster, and incomplete runs follow the prespecified failure policy rather than being dropped.
The initial target is five independent searches per adaptive condition, finalized through a development-only feasibility and variance pilot.
Paired 95\% bootstrap intervals resample source groups jointly across methods.
Optimizer comparisons additionally resample searches, paired only for deliberately paired experimental blocks; scoring repeats are not independent tasks.
Reviewer dependence receives a separate cluster sensitivity check.
Report run estimates, effect sizes, and the prespecified multiplicity correction for significance claims.

\paragraph{Checkpoint assessment.}
Test is opened after search and all selection rules are frozen.
Figure~\ref{fig:c1-landscape}b uses checkpoints at 0, 10, 25, 40, 60, 80, and 100\% of the common cost cap.
The four-domain mechanism protocol additionally permits 25/50/75/100\% checkpoint summaries.
Each boundary uses the last retained $B$, carried forward after an early stop, never a Test-selected evaluator.
Additional checkpoint assessment costs are separate from search.

\subsection{Controller and measurement}
The reference controller, \texttt{bounded\_direction\_v2}, separates execution validity, Train control, and Val retention.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:era} gives the loop; numerical settings below specify the reference implementation rather than tuned optima.
\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Direction development with confirmed references and bounded exploration}
\label{alg:era}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Seed $E_0$, Train, sealed Val, frozen measurement and control settings
\State Measure $E_0$ on Val; set $B\gets E_0$; initialize history
\While{search resources remain}
  \State Propose direction $d$; initialize $R=W=B$, scope, and direction limits
  \State Freeze direction panel $\mathcal D_d$ from the selected Train cases
  \While{$d$ is active and its resources remain}
    \State Save parent $P\gets W$; submit candidate $C$ and pre-execution predictions
    \State Validate the edit and measure $P,C,R$ under matched Train conditions
    \If{required executions are incomplete}
      \State Record the interruption; \Return the last valid $B$
    \EndIf
    \State Record effective changes, declared-check outcomes, gains, and declared contrast-case losses
    \If{$C$ qualifies for the single fresh confirmation measurement}
      \State Remeasure all compared evaluators on the same Train cases under its frozen scope
      \State Use the fresh measurement, including check outcomes, for the decision
    \EndIf
    \State Confirm $R=W=C$, stage $W=C$, or roll back $W=R$
    \State Apply the decision rules below; update pending age, rejection count, and scope
    \State Retain observations and interpretations separately, including unsuccessful attempts
    \If{a checkpoint is due and $W$ is execution-valid}
      \State Compare $W,B$ on full Val; apply Equation~\ref{eq:acceptance}
      \State Release only promotion and retained score; leave Train states unchanged
    \EndIf
    \State End $d$ on the evolution agent's recorded decision or a direction limit
  \EndWhile
  \State Perform the scheduled exit checkpoint; archive the direction before reset
\EndWhile
\State Complete any outstanding final checkpoint; freeze and return $B$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}



\paragraph{State and declarations.}
Each direction starts from $R=W=B$.
Confirmation updates $R$ and $W$, staging changes only $W$, and rollback restores the exact $R$ while retaining diagnostics.
Val changes only $B$.
The agent maintains the target failure and intended correction; changing the correction opens a new direction.
The edit identifies changed components and intended effects.
Numerical gain predictions and intermediate checks are optional and cannot be relabeled after measurement.
Numerical predictions identify the metric, reference, cohort, and units; cross-component edits are measured as bundles unless separate interventions are valid.
A scaffold additionally declares an intended downstream use and intermediate success condition, without requiring integration on the immediately next revision.

Intermediate checks specify a case, structured field, and predicate.
Eligible predictions must hold on every repeat, with at least one predicted field changing or node being created on every repeat.
Configuration, node calls, and evidence artifacts distinguish unrealized edits from produced-but-unused evidence; fulfilled checks do not alone establish preference gain.
Reason-text-only, final-score-only, purely semantic, and all-preservation checks remain unresolved.
Failed checks prohibit confirmation and staging; unresolved checks prohibit confirmation.
An absent check is not a passed check and does not veto supported gain.
A scaffold needs passed intermediate checks; an inert scaffold additionally needs valid preflight.

\paragraph{Measurement identity and panel.}
The immediate parent $P=W$ and candidate $C$ use matched cases, scoring, resources, and unaffected inputs.
Cache identity covers the complete evaluator, dependencies, inputs, model/tools, scoring configuration, and replicate; replay is not a fresh repeat.
Required incomplete seed or candidate measurements interrupt search and retain the last valid $B$; the reference runtime has no automatic evidence-recovery policy.
The fixed direction panel $\mathcal D_d$ contains the first at most eight selected Train IDs.
The default local roster has 28 cases, including up to four targets and eight sampled contrasts, with additional Train cases filling the remainder.
Missing panel cases are added, so the roster can exceed 28.
Compare $C$ with $P$ on the full roster and with $R$ on the panel.
Sampled contrasts are not automatically declared protected cases.

\paragraph{Gain, contrast-case losses, and uncertainty.}
For each case $i$ and evaluator $E$, let $o_{i,r}(E)$ be one if repeat $r$ ranks the human-preferred output strictly higher, and zero for a wrong ordering or tie.
Unavailable executions have no correctness value.
With $m$ complete repeats, $\widehat p_i(E)$ is their mean.
The implementation uses the sum $G_{\mathcal C}=|\mathcal C|\widehat g_{\mathcal C}$, equivalent to Equation~\ref{eq:paired-gain} after rescaling.
Its empirical uncertainty buffer for comparing $E$ and $F$ is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{SE}_{\mathcal C}(E,F)
 =\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal C}
   \bigl(s_i^2(E)+s_i^2(F)\bigr)\right]^{1/2},
 \label{eq:repeat-buffer}
\end{equation}
where $s_i^2$ is the within-case sample variance across repeats.
The gain is paired by case, not by arbitrarily aligned stochastic repeat slots.
Independent permutations of repeat order therefore leave every comparison unchanged.

The one-standard-error buffer is a decision heuristic, not a confidence interval or significance test.
Finite identical observations yield zero empirical variance, not proof that true execution noise is absent.
Shared upstream evidence and correlated model calls limit the independent-arm approximation.
These Train comparisons do not estimate generalization to unseen inputs.
The revision may explicitly declare contrast cases whose ordering accuracy should not decline.
These declarations define the guarded cases; sampled contrasts are not automatically guarded.
Declared contrast cases are checked individually: a negative change in any such case's mean correctness prevents confirmation.
Margins, intermediate outputs, ranking flips, score variation, calls, latency, and failures remain separate diagnostics.
Neither a wider score gap nor better evidence coverage is sufficient for reference confirmation.

\paragraph{Fresh confirmation.}
The initial diagnostic allocation is two repeats per evaluator and case.
Positive panel gain against $R$ within its uncertainty buffer qualifies for one fresh measurement only when execution is effective and complete, local and declared contrast gains are nonnegative, and declared checks pass or are absent.
Remeasure $P,C,R$ on the same cases with the same repeat count.
Fresh outcomes and checks replace, rather than supplement, the initial decision measurement.
Equivalent execution identities cannot request repeated confirmation draws.
Judgment is rerun while task analysis stays fixed (Appendix~\ref{app:synthesis}).

\paragraph{Train decision rules.}
\label{app:direction-decisions}
The following rules apply in order after completeness and effective-change checks.
A failed declared mechanism check rejects the candidate without staging it.
Except for a preflight-validated scaffold, an ineffective edit is also rejected.

First, supported harm relative to either $P$ or $R$ triggers rollback to $R$ and narrows the scope by one level.
Harm means at least two repeats with $G<-\operatorname{SE}$, or the stop-loss condition $G+\operatorname{SE}\leq-2$ correct pairs.
Second, confirmation requires at least two repeats, panel gain $G_{\mathcal D_d}(C,R)>\operatorname{SE}_{\mathcal D_d}(C,R)$, no declared contrast-case regression in either comparison, nonnegative local mean gain, and no failed or unresolved declared check.
Confirmation sets $R=W=C$.
An absent check does not veto this measured improvement, but is not recorded as a passed mechanism prediction.

Otherwise, a valid unresolved ordinary edit can remain provisional; an effective scaffold additionally needs passed intermediate checks.
Staging sets $W=C$ without moving $R$.
The exploratory chain permits two charged provisional steps.
A third unresolved step restores $W=R$ and records expiration, rather than restarting the window.
Confirmation or rollback resets pending age.
Unexecuted generation failures retain $W$ and pending age, subject to the separate failure limit.
Comparing every candidate against $R$ as well as $P$ prevents accumulated losses from disappearing as the parent changes.

A charged rejection restores $W=R$ and clears the provisional window; an uncharged generation retry retains the previous $W$ and pending age.
Three consecutive charged rejections end the direction; confirmation or provisional continuation resets that rejection count.
The default direction limit is 18 charged steps.
The evolution agent can also end a direction with a recorded reason based on accumulated diagnostics.
These bounds constrain the duration of exploration, not the correctness of its semantic interpretation.
Rejection does not itself prescribe a new direction, and a Val non-promotion does not enter these rules.

\paragraph{Scope adaptation.}
Permitted edits have three levels: one component, multiple components within one functional group, and coordinated changes across groups.
Scope constrains each edit from $W$, not cumulative distance from $R$.
The initial level is two; two consecutive supported reference updates expand it by one, while supported harm contracts it by one, within levels one to three.
The 24,000-character guard and calls are resource constraints, not semantic distances.
This categorical policy draws on trust-region adaptation \citep{conn2000trust}; it does not establish the local-model assumptions of stochastic convergence results \citep{bandeira2014probabilistic,chen2018storm,blanchet2019trust}.

\paragraph{Val retention.}
The reference cadence is every three charged steps, plus direction-exit and final checkpoints.
Compare eligible $W$ and $B$ on complete frozen Val under identical scoring and coverage requirements:
\begin{equation}
 B\leftarrow
 \begin{cases}
 W,&\widehat J_{\mathrm{Val}}(W)-\widehat J_{\mathrm{Val}}(B)\geq\tau_{\mathrm{Val}},\\
 B,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:acceptance}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\mathrm{Val}}>0$ is prespecified.
Only promotion and retained score are released; no Val result changes Train states or direction control.
Restricted feedback follows the motivation of the Ladder \citep{blum2015ladder}, without inheriting its theoretical bound.
Reserve the last eligible $W$ for the scheduled final comparison before reset; an incomplete comparison retains the last valid $B$.

\subsection{Reference evaluator}
\label{app:synthesis}
The concrete evaluator implements task analysis ($A$), judgment ($J$), and deterministic synthesis ($S$).
Task analysis sees the task and permitted sources, not the evaluated output.
Judgment inspects one output through LLMs, tools, subagents, and routes, producing structured evidence.
The A/J/S v1 synthesis uses atomic judgments and four criterion ratings with a weighted mean; its separate holistic diagnostic score does not enter that mean.
These components instantiate the generic revision space, not an additional optimization contribution.

Editing synthesis reuses compatible analysis/judgment outputs; editing judgment invalidates its dependent outputs while holding task analysis fixed; editing analysis invalidates dependent judgments.
Train confirmation therefore measures judgment variation conditional on shared analysis.
Frozen Val/Test evaluation remains end-to-end.
Train-derived examples cannot contain case-ID lookups or whole-case score tables.

\subsection{Search ablations}
\label{app:ablations}
Search and mining diagnostics use SV4, IG1, TG1, and AR1, without changing their C1 weights.
All search arms share selected feedback, seed, model, scoring, Val access, and cost except for the declared intervention.
Table~\ref{tab:search-results} supplements Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}a with absolute accuracy and revisions per direction.

\paragraph{Policy controls.}
History-based search can replace the correction after each revision while retaining diagnostics.
A switch initializes $R=W=B$, a new panel, and initial scope; continuation keeps the common controller.
Fixed patience uses $k\in\{2,4,6\}$ attempts selected under a shared Train-only tuning allowance.
The agent cannot voluntarily end early, but shared rejection, integrity, and resource limits remain active.
Immediate branching ends after the first execution-valid nonconfirming candidate and applies the same new-direction initialization.
These contrasts include the initialization consequences of switching, not just a change of direction identifier.

\paragraph{Information and state controls.}
Reduced feedback retains aggregate gain, loss, uncertainty, checks, transitions, and cost while withholding case-level traces.
No retained history exposes only the current diagnostic record, removing earlier records and summaries from every proposer context.
Both retain all controller checks and charge actual context/proposal costs.
No staging requires reference confirmation before advancing $W$; fixed scope removes adaptive scope changes; text-only EvalOpt matches SkillOpt's edit fields.
Shared repair allowances and Val cadence remain unchanged.
Fresh online searches, not replay alone, test these interventions.

\subsection{Trajectory assessment}
\label{app:analysis-details}
Sample directions by prespecified outcome strata and stable IDs, including failed and exhausted directions.
Review semantic continuity independently of direction IDs.
For each selected trajectory, link the earlier failure, realized/unused evidence, next edit, $R/W/B$ transition, and cost.
Separate observations from agent interpretations, allow multiple or unknown causes, and retain attempts with no follow-up.
Report censoring and cost on directions that never promote $B$.
These descriptions complement terminal controlled comparisons rather than establishing causality from a later successful revision.

\subsection{Reward-guided training}
\label{app:training}
C3 requires separately authorized training and independent assessment.
Arms share the Base checkpoint, architecture, LoRA/GRPO settings, inputs, tools, update budget, and auxiliary rewards; only the frozen reward evaluator differs.
Both rewards score a common starting-policy pool for variance, ties, validity, and sensitivity to length or unsupported detail, under development-frozen preflight thresholds and stopping rules.

Only the preflight pool is shared: once policies diverge, each samples its own online trajectories.
After prespecified checkpoint selection, new blinded judgments on unseen inputs compare EvalOpt-reward with Seed-reward and both with Base, including regressions and reserved attributes.
Reward increases alone do not establish quality gains.
Policy checkpoints, optimizer/KL settings, reward scales, update counts, and independent outcomes remain to be established.
