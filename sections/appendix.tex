\section{Domains, data sources, and study readiness}
\label{app:domains}

A \emph{domain} is a collection of tasks sharing core quality dimensions, their priorities, the overall decision rule, and the human annotation protocol.
The experimental unit is a separately adapted evaluator within one such standard.
Table~\ref{tab:domains} lists 22 candidate domains in four families; inclusion does not establish experimental readiness.
The initial core candidates are TD-SV4, TD-IG1, TD-TG1, and TD-AR1.
They are priorities, not completed experiments or a commitment to fill missing domains with proxy labels.
A domain enters the final comparison only after its data, standard, executable interface, and independent test are frozen.
All admitted domains must be reported regardless of their results.

\paragraph{Within-domain assessment.}
Train, terminal Val, and final Test contain different original-input/source groups sampled under the same fixed quality standard.
This is an independent within-domain test, not a claim that arbitrary distribution shifts are solved.
The test is sampled without consulting mining scores and is not restricted to disagreement cases.
Its selection rules, generator mixture, and duplicate thresholds are frozen before adaptation.
All derivatives of the same document, website/template, image cluster, or research topic remain together at the declared dependence level.
An optional source or generator shift must name what changes and verify that the human standard remains comparable.
Harder cases alone do not define a distinct domain.

\section{Human feedback and preference mining}
\label{app:annotation}

Human feedback has four levels: $H_0$ gives overall preferences; $H_1$ gives dimensional preferences; $H_2$ identifies error type, severity, and location; $H_3$ optionally supplies a free-form rationale.
An $H_0$-only corpus remains usable, but it supplies neither human dimensional judgments nor human explanations.
New labels are not assumed to be more reliable than reused labels.
Both feedback routes provide the same interface to evolution while preserving the source of each label (Table~\ref{tab:human-provenance}).
A record can be written as
\begin{equation}
 h=(x,y_a,y_b,q,p,a,\nu),
 \label{eq:human}
\end{equation}
where $q$ is overall quality or a dimension, $p$ is a preference, tie, or abstention, $a$ records annotator agreement, and $\nu$ records provenance.

\paragraph{Label-blind selection.}
Deduplicate and partition original-input groups before mining.
Freeze signal definitions and source mappings, select the random anchor without labels, and use only authorized anchor feedback for any human reliability qualification.
Freeze the targeted sampling policy before revealing the remaining selected labels.
For existing datasets, replay exactly this label-access order.
The random sampling arm includes its anchor within its uniform budget, not as extra data.
Both routes retain the full unselected pool for accounting; the miner never sees final-test labels.
Human qualification is not inferred from agreement with a model-annotated pilot.

\paragraph{Implemented conflict ranking.}
Preference mining computes structural disagreement without reading the pair's human preference.
Outputs are grouped by their original task input and deduplicated before comparison.
For a pair $r=(x,y_a,y_b)$, signal $j$ supplies a preference sign $u_j(r)\in\{-1,0,+1,\bot\}$ from its own score difference and declared tie margin.
Zero denotes a tie and $\bot$ missing or abstaining output; raw scores from different signals are never subtracted.
Signals require executable sources with compatible inputs; provenance, repeated-run variation, and missingness are recorded separately.
We freeze a many-to-many mapping $M_{jq}$ from signal $j$ to dimension $q$ using its definition and measurement procedure before reading preference labels.
A mixed score may connect to several dimensions, but remains one shared signal.
Signals sharing a model, training labels, or underlying metric are assigned source groups so that duplicated prompts do not become independent votes.
Metrics reserved for final independent assessment remain outside mining, evaluator tools, critique, and reward.

The ranking distinguishes three cases.
\emph{Within-dimension disagreement} occurs when signals from distinct source groups favor opposite outputs on the same criterion.
\emph{Overall--dimension disagreement} occurs when an overall signal opposes a dimensional signal.
\emph{Cross-dimensional trade-offs} occur when dimension-level preferences favor different outputs; these are not contradictory measurements of one criterion.
Writing their indicators as $I_{\mathrm{within},q}$, $I_{\mathrm{overall},q}$, and $I_{\mathrm{tradeoff}}$, the implemented priority is
\begin{equation}
 P(r)=2\sum_q I_{\mathrm{within},q}(r)
      +\sum_q I_{\mathrm{overall},q}(r)
      +I_{\mathrm{tradeoff}}(r).
 \label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
Pairs are ranked by decreasing $P(r)$, with stable pair identifiers breaking ties.
The coefficients define a structural ranking, not estimated signal reliability or a probability of evaluation error.
In particular, $P(r)=0$ can reflect missing signals or ties rather than agreement, and agreement itself need not be correct.
Overall--dimension comparisons retain whether their signals share a source group; they are not automatically independent evidence.
Each source group contributes at most one preference sign to a dimension's aggregate preference, with opposing within-group signs yielding abstention.
Missing outputs and small-margin ties remain distinct from opposing preferences.

For repeated model signals, the pairwise signal margin is the median of same-repeat score differences between the two artifacts; variation and sign changes are retained.
This differs from Equation~\ref{eq:measurement}, which first takes each artifact's median final score and then compares them.
A missing required signal repeat makes that evaluation signal unavailable for mining.
Deterministic metrics use declared ranges and orientations, not ranges fitted to the output pool or human labels.
The random anchor is selected by original-input hashes before scoring, preserving output groups.
Label-blind execution does not establish label-blind upstream cohort selection; historical data must also document how the output pool was chosen.

\paragraph{Human qualification and implementation boundary.}
The implemented miner supplies the structural queue and its source-dependence records.
The full preference-mining protocol additionally requires human-annotated anchors to qualify signals before targeted annotation; cross-domain human-reliability calibration and the equal-budget advantage remain unverified.
Model-annotated pilot anchors measure agreement with that model, not human reliability.
The annotation fraction, qualification thresholds, and targeted policy must be frozen for the human study, rather than inherited silently from a pilot.
The feedback table $Z$ is a logical join over feedback, artifact, signal, and execution records; absent human dimensions or rationales remain absent.


\paragraph{Source readiness.}
A signal is marked executable only after its input contract, output orientation, dependencies, and failure behavior are verified.
A publication, URL, or repeated prompt does not establish executable or independent evidence.
Report missing dimensions and correlated source groups rather than creating artificial votes.
External metrics reserved for assessment must not enter mining or the evolved evaluator.

Only the authorized optimization process sees proposal-Train labels and traces.
Automated tools can inspect the artifact; they cannot retrieve held-out answers or invoke reserved assessment metrics.

Published optimizers may retain their native development population or selection rules within this common access boundary.
Their validation interface maps to development data, not to the locked final test.
If an adaptation needs per-example feedback that the shared interface does not expose, it must use proposal Train or be reported as an unmatched condition.
The terminal evaluator is selected by the declared Train-only rule before terminal Val is opened.
Interrupted runs retain their failures and incurred costs; they do not receive a fresh selection opportunity.

\paragraph{Agreement estimator.}
Use the repeated-score protocol of the deployed evaluator, requesting $R\geq3$ executions per artifact and freezing $R$, precision, retries, and cache identity before measurement.
Let $P_g$ be the strict human preferences within original-input group $g$.
For complete executions,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bar s_\theta(x,y)&=\median_{r=1,\ldots,R}s_\theta^{(r)}(x,y),\\
 v_{gj}&=\ind[\bar s_\theta(x_g,y_{gj}^{+})>\bar s_\theta(x_g,y_{gj}^{-})],\\
 \widehat J(\theta)&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal G|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}
       \frac{1}{|P_g|}\sum_{j\in P_g}v_{gj}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
Input groups receive equal weight; predicted ties count as incorrect.
Human ties do not create strict preference labels.
Groups without a strict preference retain a separate count and do not enter this endpoint.
The native development proxy can use one fixed pair per input; final evaluation need not use that reduced proxy.
A reused artifact keeps its frozen repeated scores across all comparisons.

\paragraph{Missing execution and coverage.}
Fix weights $w_{gj}=1/(|\mathcal G||P_g|)$ before execution.
Let $c_{gj}$ indicate complete scoring of both artifacts, $M_{\mathrm{cov}}=\sum w_{gj}c_{gj}$, and $K_{\mathrm{corr}}=\sum w_{gj}c_{gj}v_{gj}$.
Report conditional agreement $K_{\mathrm{corr}}/M_{\mathrm{cov}}$, weighted coverage, raw counts, and
\begin{equation}
 [K_{\mathrm{corr}},\,K_{\mathrm{corr}}+1-M_{\mathrm{cov}}],
 \label{eq:missing}
\end{equation}
the possible full-cohort agreement range under arbitrary missing outcomes.
This is not a confidence interval.
Main agreement contrasts use a common complete cohort across the compared methods, with the requested-cohort bounds alongside them; method-specific complete cohorts are supplementary.
Missing required repeats or tool results remain missing, rather than being replaced by successful retries selected after seeing their score.
Valid evidence of a low-quality artifact is an outcome, not an execution failure.

\paragraph{Selection and top-region judgments.}
Freeze a common output pool size for the main selection cohort; the initial design proposes $N=4$, subject to data readiness.
Other $N$ values are separate sensitivity analyses.
Complete human rankings, including declared ties, identify the set of top-ranked outputs.
Best-of-$N$ succeeds when the evaluator selects a member of that set.
Resolve evaluator score ties by a fixed output-identifier rule independent of human rank.
With human ties, define regret as the number of outputs strictly preferred to the selected output divided by $N-1$; for strict rankings this is $(\operatorname{rank}(\hat y)-1)/(N-1)$ for the selected output $\hat y$.
For a human-ranked group without a valid evaluator selection, count selection as unsuccessful and regret as one, and additionally report complete-case sensitivity.
Top-region agreement uses strict pairs touching the human top-two region, including any ties at its boundary.
No-consensus or insufficient human rankings are reported separately and never supplied by the evaluator.
Pair-only data cannot establish any of these multi-output endpoints.

\paragraph{Aggregation and uncertainty.}
Freeze the admitted domain set per endpoint before outcomes are opened and use equal-domain means.
Report the domain count, input/source groups, pairs, outputs, ranking pool size, and completion for every number.
Separate reused-label and new-label results before combining compatible endpoints.
Do not pool incomparable quality metrics or replace an unavailable domain after observing its performance.

The main performance estimate averages independently completed searches, not the best search selected retrospectively.
Static methods have no search-seed variance.
The initial target is five independent searches per adaptive condition, with the final count and sample sizes frozen after a development-only feasibility and variance pilot.
Report all run estimates when the available count is too small for stable intervals.
For fixed evaluators, compute paired 95\% bootstrap intervals by resampling original-input/source groups jointly across methods, retaining all their outputs.
For optimizer comparisons, additionally resample independent search runs, paired only when the experimental blocks were deliberately paired; otherwise resample method runs independently.
The same input resample is used across methods and runs.
Scoring repeats do not increase the number of independent tasks or searches.
If shared reviewers introduce additional dependence, retain reviewer identifiers and report reviewer-cluster or crossed-cluster sensitivity rather than treating votes as independent.

Predeclare one principal program comparison (IterEval versus GEPA), the Seed contrast, and the direction-versus-history contrast.
Report paired effect sizes and intervals for all other comparisons, with a prespecified multiplicity correction for any family of significance claims.
Select sample sizes from a target minimum effect and development-only variance, not from a desired test outcome.
A nonsignificant difference does not establish equivalence.
Bootstrap intervals on one final evaluator do not establish search stability.

\paragraph{Independent checkpoints.}
Record each search's best accepted program at fixed fractions of the cost cap, proposed as 25, 50, 75, and 100\%.
Use the last completed selection before each boundary.
Assess these frozen checkpoints on Test only after all search and configuration choices are complete.
A run ending early carries its last accepted program forward, with its stopping reason retained.
Do not interpolate unexecuted programs or use the test curves to pick another final evaluator.

\paragraph{Learned signal fusion.}
A simple reproducible control fits a regularized pairwise logistic model to differences in frozen per-output signal features on proposal Train.
Use its pointwise linear score for final ranking.
Freeze feature orientation, normalization, missing-value handling, regularization search, and dimensional-label use before testing.
Fit transformations only on training data.
A monotonic remapping of one score cannot improve its ranking except through altered ties; it is not a meaningful substitute for this multi-signal control.

\paragraph{SkillOpt.}
Use the official text/skill optimization path, not a custom program-evolution extension bearing its name.
The candidate skill is inserted into a fixed seed interface, with tools and routes unchanged.
Retain rejected edits and native longer-timescale updates; do not describe the baseline as memoryless or assume every failed edit causes a semantic restart.
Map its internal selection feedback to development data and charge reflection and selection calls.
Compare it additionally with IterEval restricted to the identical text fields, evidence inputs, and edit envelope.
This same-space contrast is necessary to attribute a difference to optimization rather than broader program access.

\paragraph{GEPA and Meta-Harness.}
Current GEPA interfaces support program optimization; restricting it to prompts would not test the closest alternative.
Use a declared whole-program or multicomponent representation with the official optimizer, returning scores and authorized trajectories through its evaluator adapter.
Preserve the configured population, candidate-selection, and merge mechanisms, and distinguish this program adaptation from the original paper's particular experiments.
Meta-Harness must retain its full-history code and trace access.
Its released examples assume a particular coding-agent wrapper; replace only the transport needed for the matched optimizer model, preserving logging and native search decisions.
If model access cannot be matched, place that run in an explicitly unmatched supplementary comparison rather than the main controlled result.

\paragraph{Additional comparators.}
AFlow is the first optional structured workflow-search baseline because it provides an explicit workflow search implementation \citep{zhang2025aflow}.
Its multimodal task adapter must be validated rather than assumed from text/code examples.
ADAS and DGM are relevant architecture/code-evolution alternatives \citep{hu2025adas,zhang2025dgm}, but need not all enter the main table if the closest full-program comparisons are already covered.
HarnessBank and SkillHEX remain related methods unless an official executable release and compatible evaluation interface are verified \citep{luo2026harnessbank,feng2026skillhex}.
A paper or abstract is not sufficient to mark a baseline implemented.

Evaluator fine-tuning is an optional separate study on an open-weight backbone, not a required row across domains with incompatible model access.
It follows the trained-judge direction exemplified by Prometheus and CritiqueLLM \citep{kim2024prometheus,ke2024critiquellm}.
Match the initial checkpoint, feedback, observations, and training-selection access; report training compute separately.
No additional teacher labels are free.
This trains the evaluator, unlike C3, which trains the generator.
A query-specific rubric model trained for research reports \citep{lv2026queryrubrics} is not automatically an ideation evaluator.

\paragraph{Resource controls.}
The primary evolution cap is total model-and-tool cost at a frozen price schedule.
Count proposal and reflection tokens, diagnostics, rejected and invalid attempts, repeated candidate measurements, retries, and the reserved terminal assessment.
Track actual tokens, calls, accelerator time where applicable, wall time, and tool-specific charges separately.
Different attempt counts under equal cost are outcomes.
Mining and annotation are separate cost categories; the end-to-end comparison includes both.
Deploy every final evaluator under the same permitted input, context, tool, time, and token caps.
Report actual deployment cost instead of assuming that an equal cap produces equal use.
Fixed final evaluation costs do not buy additional search.
Table~\ref{tab:cost-results} records these distinctions.

\paragraph{Code-grounded cost scenario.}
For the current illustration, we estimate model cost for one successful pointwise evaluation, not a full deployment bill.
The inspected Seed configurations use 2, 4, 4, and 5 specialist calls in SV4, IG1, TG1, and AR1, respectively, plus 0, 3, 3, and 5 routing calls and one synthesis call in each domain.
Fixed uses one holistic call; ``fixed'' describes its unchanged prompt, not zero inference cost.
For Fusion, we assume the features are all Seed specialist signals, generate them at their full cost, and replace synthesis with a local linear combination.
The available SV4 SkillOpt program retains the Seed structure and prompt lengths; the available IterEval program changes the synthesis prompt from 427 to 5,088 characters without adding calls.
For other method--domain combinations, we use Seed's call topology and request-length profile without predicting their semantic edits.
Agreement values remain independent layout fixtures, not measurements of these inspected programs; these conditional costs do not establish an empirical ranking of optimizers.

We sum input tokens plus four times output tokens, normalize within each domain by Seed, and average the four ratios.
Code and configuration text use a characters-divided-by-four token approximation, including scoring schemas.
Assumed artifact-input lengths are 4,000, 250, 3,000, and 8,000 tokens; image-token allowances are 2,000, 1,000, 0, and 0; synthesis contexts are 500, 150, 500, and 1,500 tokens in the same domain order.

\paragraph{Investigation and candidate construction.}
The evolution agent reads original proposal-Train artifacts, installed component instructions, execution traces, and relevant history through a tool-driven editing session.
It can request additional authorized proposal-Train measurements or fixed-input component probes.
The submission contains actual edits, a short intent, and references to inspected training cases.
Predicted orderings, contrast cases, and intermediate checks are optional declarations, not controller-inferred scientific predictions.
The controller records the exact parent, installed program, and measured outcomes.
Local diagnostic success is not an admission requirement for the common development measurement.
The state $(B,W,d,z)$ in Equation~\ref{eq:search-state} summarizes these persisted records.
The direction identifier links its plan, root errors, previous trials, and unresolved questions.
Root errors are evaluation errors present in the program from which the direction starts.
Starting a new direction does not delete earlier direction histories.
The native patch interface enforces the direction identifier, but allows the intent to be revised; it does not enforce an invariant target set or semantic correction.

An elementary edit changes one component; a connected candidate can assemble a tool, its route, and its consumer against a fixed parent.
Only the complete candidate is executed and measured, not its partially assembled versions.
Changed tools must be connected to valid routes, although valid routing alone does not establish correct use of their outputs.
Interface validity, fixed-model constraints, and execution limits remain mandatory; edit-character counts are recorded as guidance rather than a hard acceptance or stopping rule.
Unchanged programs and verified historical replays are distinguished from fresh trials, and failed calls remain in the cost record.

\paragraph{Diagnostic results.}
The diagnostic state retains execution records and repaired, unchanged, regressed, or unknown local outcomes, alongside the agent's intended changes, interpretations, and unresolved questions.
Recorded outputs are distinguished from explanations of why they occurred.
These descriptions need not form a complete structured checklist for every candidate.
Human preferences establish the target ordering; the agent must investigate why the program fails to reproduce it.
Fixed-input probes compare supported specialist or synthesis variants while holding their upstream inputs fixed.
Such probes bind their observations to the exact tested candidate and inputs; changing a tool, route, or topology requires the corresponding complete execution.
An optional exact intermediate check records whether a declared field changed to its expected value on at least one complete repeat.
All repeat-level matches, contradictions, and unknowns remain visible.
A semantic expectation is not automatically verified, and a changed recorded field is not proof of a correct causal explanation or improved overall judgment.

\paragraph{Candidate acceptance and the next working program.}
Candidate acceptance requires complete, comparable execution, a strict development-agreement gain over the best evaluator, nondecreasing coverage, and the prespecified worst-stratum guard.
These are development selection rules rather than statistical significance claims.
Within a continuing direction, the parent rule determines the next working program.
Using the candidate as the next parent first requires known, complete, comparable local and development outcomes, including comparisons against both the parent and best program.
Subject to that requirement, accepted candidates and candidates with no observed damage can become the next parent.
For this decision, damage includes a local ordering regression, deterioration on a declared protection case, or more regressed than corrected sealed-Train comparisons against either the parent or the best evaluator.
An unaccepted candidate with damage is eligible only when fresh measurement supplies new progress and the declared protection cases do not deteriorate.

Qualifying progress is either a first-time repair of an error present at the direction's root, still wrong in the parent, or a newly observed, predeclared exact intermediate-field change.
Previously credited repairs and checks are retained across attempts, so breaking and repairing an already corrected case cannot renew this allowance.
A missing protected case is unknown, not preserved.
When the candidate fails these checks, the next parent returns to the pre-update best program, even if the candidate has just been accepted as the new best.
The candidate and its diagnostics remain available.
Keeping an intermediate candidate never changes the recorded best score.

\paragraph{Continuing and ending a direction.}
In the native editing session, the agent can request diagnostics, submit a candidate, repair an invalid submission, or end the active direction.
Ending a direction uses the native \texttt{pause} action, which submits a no-change record with a nonempty reason.
The session instruction asks for an unresolved question and why no different informative test is ready; the parser does not require a particular reason category or verify the explanation.
The controller enforces the parent rule, validity, and resource limits without judging whether a direction is unhelpful.
Revisions continue within the active direction until the agent ends it or an execution or resource limit stops the search.
The history is retained in either case.
An ending explanation may concern implementation problems or results suggesting that an implemented correction does not address the target errors.
That explanation is the agent's assessment, not an automatically verified conclusion.
The current implementation therefore does not prevent an agent from ending a direction early or guarantee a minimum exploration depth.
One unsuccessful implementation, a nonsignificant difference, or budget exhaustion alone does not establish that the direction is unhelpful.
In the default implementation, edit size and cumulative direction edits are guidance, not a hard direction budget.
Edit-character counts, replacement fractions, and component counts are advisory.
Configured proposal opportunities, session action bounds, and execution limits constrain the run; they should not be described as an implemented fixed depth per direction.
An integrity interruption does not trigger the normal terminal-validation procedure.

\paragraph{Terminal assessment and attribution.}
The terminal shortlist includes eligible, previously accepted, complete programs with positive development gain over the seed and is ordered using Train evidence alone.
Its membership and order are frozen before Val access.
Terminal Val does not rerank the shortlist or guide another proposal.
With no eligible improvement, the seed is retained without an additional Val query.
Fresh system confirmation and independent held-out assessment remain separate from adaptive development.

Connected edits share one parent and one measured outcome, so a bundle's gain is not independent evidence for each edit.
Component attribution requires paired interventions with the remaining interfaces fixed, such as tool on/off or evolved versus seed synthesis.
If removing a dependency makes the program invalid, the comparison must report that limitation rather than substitute an unrelated whole-program control.
Complete prompt templates, interaction traces, candidate changes, and execution settings belong in the reproducibility record rather than the main algorithm.

\section{Semantic synthesis and anchored scoring}
\label{app:synthesis}

The default fusion procedure forms an overall judgment from specialist findings; a declared numerical rule is an explicit alternative.
Each specialist produces findings with identifiers, supporting evidence, dimensions, and uncertainty, as well as its own rating and reason.
The full specialist trace remains available for diagnosis, but the synthesizer's default input omits the structured specialist score, rating, and score scale.
It retains the findings, reasons, and scope of each specialist and applies its own overall scale.
This input separation does not remove value judgments expressed in the reasons.

The synthesizer groups findings by their consequences for quality, including strengths, weaknesses, mixed effects, and uncertainty.
Every finding reference must occur in exactly one group.
This accounting rule makes omitted or multiply referenced findings detectable; it does not guarantee correct semantic grouping.
Duplicate descriptions should not become independent penalties, and favorable style should not cancel a factual error when the domain standard prioritizes faithfulness.

Let $a_k<a_{k+1}$ be adjacent overall anchors and $\lambda\in[0,1]$ locate quality between them.
The resulting scalar is
\begin{equation}
 s=(1-\lambda)a_k+\lambda a_{k+1}.
 \label{eq:anchors}
\end{equation}
The anchors have domain-specific verbal meanings; specialist scores with different meanings are not averaged.
Reference completeness, semantic correctness, and calibration require separate checks.
A synthetic arithmetic example uses $a_k=50$, $a_{k+1}=75$, and $\lambda=0.4$, giving $s=60$.
These numbers illustrate interpolation, not a universal scale or an evaluator result.

Null, negative, and incomplete outcomes each have distinct reporting statuses.
