\section{Domain panorama and evaluation standards}
\label{app:domains}

A \emph{domain} is a collection of tasks sharing core quality dimensions, their priorities, the overall decision rule, and the human annotation protocol.
A domain is defined by its evaluation standard rather than its artifact type.
For example, screenshot-conditioned webpage reconstruction and specification-conditioned webpage generation require different standards.
A change of source distribution that preserves the standard is instead a within-domain distribution shift.
Table~\ref{tab:domains} lists 22 candidate domains in four families; inclusion does not establish experimental readiness.
Closed tasks whose complete quality criterion is an executable postcondition fall outside this scope.

The research scope is organized by evaluation standard, not by file format.
Table~\ref{tab:domains} retains all 22 candidate domains. Its dimensions are a
starting taxonomy to be refined from domain evaluation knowledge before selecting
datasets. Each instantiated domain must additionally fix priorities, decision
rules, and an annotation protocol. Inclusion in this panorama does not establish
data readiness, an implemented adapter, or empirical transfer.
Figure~\ref{fig:overview}a gives representative artifacts; the table makes
their differing evaluation criteria explicit.

A benchmark collection spanning several standards remains a dataset collection,
not an additional domain. Research execution with a complete executable
postcondition is excluded, while ideation, study design, interpretation, writing,
and critique remain open-ended evaluation problems.

\paragraph{Dataset roles.}
Dev supports data mining and evaluator development, ID tests unseen inputs from the development source distribution, and OOD changes source distribution while preserving the standard.
External datasets provide separately locked assessment only and do not determine semantic mappings or data mining.
Harder examples from the same source are a difficulty stratum, not a new OOD domain.
A domain selected for confirmatory execution must pass the audit below; selection does not remove unselected domains from the research scope.

\section{Feedback provenance, annotation, and readiness}
\label{app:annotation}

Existing human preferences are not a domain-admission requirement.
Their availability determines the missing data work, not the algorithm's definition.
An evaluation ecosystem can exist without a suitable preference dataset.
We distinguish \emph{reused preferences}, which test improvement on established benchmarks, from \emph{new preferences}, which test applicability when matching human judgments must first be collected.
One domain can support both settings.

The two construction settings in Table~\ref{tab:human-provenance} share the
same evaluator-evolution problem. They differ in the work needed to obtain
matched human feedback. A domain may use both settings. Direct, derived, and
materials-only provenance describe what a resource supplies; they are not
additional domain classes or alternative algorithms.

Inputs, references, editable artifacts, and automatic metrics without matched human judgments are materials only.
They may support output construction or independent assessment, but do not supply human feedback.
When $H_0$ is absent, we preserve the original task and evaluation standard, separate source groups, recover readable outputs or generate them with frozen generators, and collect the required feedback.
Preparation can precede annotation; final human-alignment claims cannot precede matched human assessment.

\paragraph{Annotation units and levels.}
One annotation unit is an original input and its anonymized output group, including permitted references.
Pairs derived from that group remain dependent.
For existing datasets, the label-access budget counts original-input groups whose preferences are revealed, not newly performed human work.
For new annotation, we match annotated groups and the requested feedback levels and rater counts, and report annotation time separately.
Expanding one ranking into several pairs does not create additional independent annotation units.
For a domain with quality dimensions $\mathcal Q_D=\{q_1,\ldots,q_m\}$, a human comparison has the core form
\begin{equation}
 h=(x,y_a,y_b,q,p,a,\nu),
 \label{eq:human}
\end{equation}
where $q$ is a dimension or overall quality, $p$ is a preference, tie, or abstention, $a$ records annotator agreement, and $\nu$ records provenance.
Human feedback has four levels: $H_0$ gives overall preferences; $H_1$ gives dimensional preferences; $H_2$ identifies error type, severity, and location; $H_3$ optionally supplies a free-form rationale.
$H_1$ is central to the dimension-guided design.
An $H_0$-only corpus remains usable, but it supplies neither human dimensional judgments nor human explanations.
The core tuple in Equation~\ref{eq:human} is retained together with dataset and license, source grouping, generator and prompt version, collection time, annotator pool, agreement, and conversion provenance.
Generator identity is recorded for audit but hidden from annotators and the deployed scorer.

The planned evolution protocol collects $H_0$ and core $H_1$, with $H_2$ on conflict and failure subsets.
$H_3$ is optional.
Two independent annotators with adjudication may support development; final ID/OOD assessment targets three independent annotators and blinded complete rankings.
Core dimensions and the tie policy are fixed in advance.
A model or an author decision is not substituted for a missing independent final annotation.
An $H_0$-only setting must be reported as such; the framework's richer interface does not establish that a run actually received $H_1$ or $H_2$.

\paragraph{Agreement, abstention, and reannotation.}
New labels are not assumed to be more reliable than reused labels. Report the
agreement statistic, its valid-response denominator, and the number of raters
per group. Abstention uses all requested ratings as its denominator, including
those excluded from valid-pair agreement. Group outcomes distinguish decisive
preference, tie, no consensus, and insufficient votes. The simulated audit in
Figure~\ref{fig:annotation-quality}a--c illustrates these separate quantities.
Individual disagreements are retained rather than filtered to match an old
winner.

\begin{samepage}
Annotators do not see model scores, method identity, or earlier winners.
A changed rubric or visible evidence defines a changed protocol; resulting
differences do not by themselves establish errors in the older labels.\par
\end{samepage}

\paragraph{Label-blind preference mining.}
Freeze domain knowledge, signal definitions, semantic mappings, and the calibration recipe before accessing preference labels.
Select a random anchor, reveal only its authorized training judgments to estimate reliability, then freeze the targeted sampling policy before opening labels in the remaining pool.
Count anchor labels toward each strategy's label-access or new-annotation budget, as applicable.
An existing labeled dataset must simulate the same access order.
The final cohort is sampled independently of conflicts and never contributes to reliability calibration.

\paragraph{Implemented conflict ranking.}
Preference mining computes structural disagreement without reading the pair's human preference.
Outputs are grouped by their original task input and deduplicated before comparison.
For a pair $r=(x,y_a,y_b)$, signal $j$ supplies a preference sign $u_j(r)\in\{-1,0,+1,\bot\}$ from its own score difference and declared tie margin.
Zero denotes a tie and $\bot$ missing or abstaining output; raw scores from different signals are never subtracted.
Signals require executable sources with compatible inputs; provenance, repeated-run variation, and missingness are recorded separately.
We freeze a many-to-many mapping $M_{jq}$ from signal $j$ to dimension $q$ using its definition and measurement procedure before reading preference labels.
A mixed score may connect to several dimensions, but remains one shared signal.
Signals sharing a model, training labels, or underlying metric are assigned source groups so that duplicated prompts do not become independent votes.
Metrics reserved for final independent assessment remain outside mining, evaluator tools, critique, and reward.

The ranking distinguishes three cases.
\emph{Within-dimension disagreement} occurs when signals from distinct source groups favor opposite outputs on the same criterion.
\emph{Overall--dimension disagreement} occurs when an overall signal opposes a dimensional signal.
\emph{Cross-dimensional trade-offs} occur when dimension-level preferences favor different outputs; these are not contradictory measurements of one criterion.
Writing their indicators as $I_{\mathrm{within},q}$, $I_{\mathrm{overall},q}$, and $I_{\mathrm{tradeoff}}$, the implemented priority is
\begin{equation}
 P(r)=2\sum_q I_{\mathrm{within},q}(r)
      +\sum_q I_{\mathrm{overall},q}(r)
      +I_{\mathrm{tradeoff}}(r).
 \label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
Pairs are ranked by decreasing $P(r)$, with stable pair identifiers breaking ties.
The coefficients define a structural ranking, not estimated signal reliability or a probability of evaluation error.
In particular, $P(r)=0$ can reflect missing signals or ties rather than agreement, and agreement itself need not be correct.
Overall--dimension comparisons retain whether their signals share a source group; they are not automatically independent evidence.
Each source group contributes at most one preference sign to a dimension's aggregate preference, with opposing within-group signs yielding abstention.
Missing outputs and small-margin ties remain distinct from opposing preferences.

For repeated model signals, the pairwise signal margin is the median of same-repeat score differences between the two artifacts; variation and sign changes are retained.
This differs from Equation~\ref{eq:measurement}, which first takes each artifact's median final score and then compares them.
A missing required signal repeat makes that evaluation signal unavailable for mining.
Deterministic metrics use declared ranges and orientations, not ranges fitted to the output pool or human labels.
The random anchor is selected by original-input hashes before scoring, preserving output groups.
Label-blind execution does not establish label-blind upstream cohort selection; historical data must also document how the output pool was chosen.

\paragraph{Human qualification and implementation boundary.}
The implemented miner supplies the structural queue and its source-dependence records.
The full preference-mining protocol additionally requires human-annotated anchors to qualify signals before targeted annotation; cross-domain human-reliability calibration and the equal-budget advantage remain unverified.
Model-annotated pilot anchors measure agreement with that model, not human reliability.
The annotation fraction, qualification thresholds, and targeted policy must be frozen for the human study, rather than inherited silently from a pilot.
The feedback table $Z$ is a logical join over feedback, artifact, signal, and execution records; absent human dimensions or rationales remain absent.

Registry entries distinguish executable, planned, and blocked sources
(Table~\ref{tab:readiness}).

is not an executed signal. Available tools begin as candidates; a route must

At deployment, the evaluator receives one candidate output and its permitted evidence; opponents, human labels, generator identity, split labels, and mining strata are excluded.

\paragraph{Training and validation.}
Proposal-Train evidence may inform evolution directions, candidates, and allocation.
Sealed Train reveals prespecified aggregate agreement, coverage, and stratum
guards without examples, labels, or per-case errors. Aggregates remain adaptive
development evidence, not a final performance estimate. The terminal shortlist
and its order are fixed from training evidence before Val access, and every
shortlisted outcome is reported. Repeated Val calls can assess stability but do
not create an independent test cohort. Interrupted searches do not obtain a new
terminal assessment merely because they stopped.

\paragraph{External metrics.}
The External Metric Reserve is frozen before search. Reserved metrics and their
equivalent wrappers are excluded from the executable tool library, router,
diagnostician, critique, reward, and checkpoint-selection criteria. If an endpoint
was used for optimization, it loses independent-assessment status and must be
replaced or explicitly reported as an optimization metric. This restriction
does not forbid using other existing metrics as tools.

\paragraph{Investigation and candidate construction.}
The evolution agent reads original proposal-Train artifacts, installed component instructions, execution traces, and relevant history through a tool-driven editing session.
It can request additional authorized proposal-Train measurements or fixed-input component probes.
The submission contains actual edits, a short intent, and references to inspected training cases.
Predicted orderings, contrast cases, and intermediate checks are optional declarations, not controller-inferred scientific predictions.
The controller records the exact parent, installed program, and measured outcomes.
Local diagnostic success is not an admission requirement for the common development measurement.
The state $(B,W,d,z)$ in Equation~\ref{eq:search-state} summarizes these persisted records.
The direction identifier links its plan, root errors, previous trials, and unresolved questions.
Root errors are evaluation errors present in the program from which the direction starts.
Starting a new direction does not delete earlier direction histories.
The native patch interface enforces the direction identifier, but allows the intent to be revised; it does not enforce an invariant target set or semantic correction.

An elementary edit changes one component; a connected candidate can assemble a tool, its route, and its consumer against a fixed parent.
Only the complete candidate is executed and measured, not its partially assembled versions.
Changed tools must be connected to valid routes, although valid routing alone does not establish correct use of their outputs.
Interface validity, fixed-model constraints, and execution limits remain mandatory; edit-character counts are recorded as guidance rather than a hard acceptance or stopping rule.
Unchanged programs and verified historical replays are distinguished from fresh trials, and failed calls remain in the cost record.

\paragraph{Diagnostic results.}
The diagnostic state retains execution records and repaired, unchanged, regressed, or unknown local outcomes, alongside the agent's intended changes, interpretations, and unresolved questions.
Recorded outputs are distinguished from explanations of why they occurred.
These descriptions need not form a complete structured checklist for every candidate.
Human preferences establish the target ordering; the agent must investigate why the program fails to reproduce it.
Fixed-input probes compare supported specialist or synthesis variants while holding their upstream inputs fixed.
Such probes bind their observations to the exact tested candidate and inputs; changing a tool, route, or topology requires the corresponding complete execution.
An optional exact intermediate check records whether a declared field changed to its expected value on at least one complete repeat.
All repeat-level matches, contradictions, and unknowns remain visible.
A semantic expectation is not automatically verified, and a changed recorded field is not proof of a correct causal explanation or improved overall judgment.

\paragraph{Candidate acceptance and the next working program.}
Candidate acceptance requires complete, comparable execution, a strict development-agreement gain over the best evaluator, nondecreasing coverage, and the prespecified worst-stratum guard.
These are development selection rules rather than statistical significance claims.
Within a continuing direction, the parent rule determines the next working program.
Using the candidate as the next parent first requires known, complete, comparable local and development outcomes, including comparisons against both the parent and best program.
Subject to that requirement, accepted candidates and candidates with no observed damage can become the next parent.
For this decision, damage includes a local ordering regression, deterioration on a declared protection case, or more regressed than corrected sealed-Train comparisons against either the parent or the best evaluator.
An unaccepted candidate with damage is eligible only when fresh measurement supplies new progress and the declared protection cases do not deteriorate.

Qualifying progress is either a first-time repair of an error present at the direction's root, still wrong in the parent, or a newly observed, predeclared exact intermediate-field change.
Previously credited repairs and checks are retained across attempts, so breaking and repairing an already corrected case cannot renew this allowance.
A missing protected case is unknown, not preserved.
When the candidate fails these checks, the next parent returns to the pre-update best program, even if the candidate has just been accepted as the new best.
The candidate and its diagnostics remain available.
Keeping an intermediate candidate never changes the recorded best score.

\paragraph{Continuing and ending a direction.}
In the native editing session, the agent can request diagnostics, submit a candidate, repair an invalid submission, or end the active direction.
Ending a direction uses the native \texttt{pause} action, which submits a no-change record with a nonempty reason.
The session instruction asks for an unresolved question and why no different informative test is ready; the parser does not require a particular reason category or verify the explanation.
The controller enforces the parent rule, validity, and resource limits without judging whether a direction is unhelpful.
Revisions continue within the active direction until the agent ends it or an execution or resource limit stops the search.
The history is retained in either case.
An ending explanation may concern implementation problems or results suggesting that an implemented correction does not address the target errors.
That explanation is the agent's assessment, not an automatically verified conclusion.
The current implementation therefore does not prevent an agent from ending a direction early or guarantee a minimum exploration depth.
One unsuccessful implementation, a nonsignificant difference, or budget exhaustion alone does not establish that the direction is unhelpful.
In the default implementation, edit size and cumulative direction edits are guidance, not a hard direction budget.
Edit-character counts, replacement fractions, and component counts are advisory.
Configured proposal opportunities, session action bounds, and execution limits constrain the run; they should not be described as an implemented fixed depth per direction.
An integrity interruption does not trigger the normal terminal-validation procedure.

\paragraph{Terminal assessment and attribution.}
The terminal shortlist includes eligible, previously accepted, complete programs with positive development gain over the seed and is ordered using Train evidence alone.
Its membership and order are frozen before Val access.
Terminal Val does not rerank the shortlist or guide another proposal.
With no eligible improvement, the seed is retained without an additional Val query.
Fresh system confirmation and independent Test/OOD assessment remain separate from adaptive development.

Connected edits share one parent and one measured outcome, so a bundle's gain is not independent evidence for each edit.
Component attribution requires paired interventions with the remaining interfaces fixed, such as tool on/off or evolved versus seed synthesis.
If removing a dependency makes the program invalid, the comparison must report that limitation rather than substitute an unrelated whole-program control.
Complete prompt templates, interaction traces, candidate changes, and execution settings belong in the reproducibility record rather than the main algorithm.

\section{Semantic synthesis and anchored scoring}
\label{app:synthesis}

The default fusion procedure forms an overall judgment from specialist findings; a declared numerical rule is an explicit alternative.
Each specialist produces findings with identifiers, supporting evidence, dimensions, and uncertainty, as well as its own rating and reason.
The full specialist trace remains available for diagnosis, but the synthesizer's default input omits the structured specialist score, rating, and score scale.
It retains the findings, reasons, and scope of each specialist and applies its own overall scale.
This input separation does not remove value judgments expressed in the reasons.

The synthesizer groups findings by their consequences for quality, including strengths, weaknesses, mixed effects, and uncertainty.
Every finding reference must occur in exactly one group.
This accounting rule makes omitted or multiply referenced findings detectable; it does not guarantee correct semantic grouping.
Duplicate descriptions should not become independent penalties, and favorable style should not cancel a factual error when the domain standard prioritizes faithfulness.

Let $a_k<a_{k+1}$ be adjacent overall anchors and $\lambda\in[0,1]$ locate quality between them.
The resulting scalar is
\begin{equation}
 s=(1-\lambda)a_k+\lambda a_{k+1}.
 \label{eq:anchors}
\end{equation}
The anchors have domain-specific verbal meanings; specialist scores with different meanings are not averaged.
Reference completeness, semantic correctness, and calibration require separate checks.
A synthetic arithmetic example uses $a_k=50$, $a_{k+1}=75$, and $\lambda=0.4$, giving $s=60$.
These numbers illustrate interpolation, not a universal scale or an evaluator result.

\section{Measurement and statistical analysis}
\label{app:measurement}

\paragraph{Agreement estimator.}
The distribution $\mathcal P_D$ in Equation~\ref{eq:objective} samples an input from domain $D$, then a strict human preference uniformly within that input's output group.
Search estimates agreement on a finite cohort, requesting $R\geq3$ executions per artifact for full-evaluator measurements.
For complete executions on input groups $\mathcal S_{\mathrm{eval}}$ with nonempty strict-preference sets $P_g$, define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bar{s}_\theta(x,y)&=\median_{r=1,\ldots,R}s_\theta^{(r)}(x,y),\quad
 v_{gj}=\ind[\bar{s}_\theta(x_g,y_{gj}^+)>\bar{s}_\theta(x_g,y_{gj}^-)],\\
 \widehat J_{\mathcal S_{\mathrm{eval}}}(\theta)&=
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal S_{\mathrm{eval}}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{eval}}}
 \frac{1}{|P_g|}\sum_{j\in P_g}v_{gj}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
Input groups receive equal weight; predicted ties count as incorrect.
The native development proxy uses one fixed pair per input and need not represent every within-group preference.

\paragraph{Completeness and denominators.}
Use the original-input weights $w_{gj}=1/(|\mathcal S_{\mathrm{eval}}||P_g|)$ from Equation~\ref{eq:measurement}, fixed before execution.
Let $c_{gj}=1$ when both artifacts have all $R$ valid complete scores, $M_{\mathrm{cov}}=\sum_{g,j}w_{gj}c_{gj}$ be covered weight, and $K_{\mathrm{corr}}=\sum_{g,j}w_{gj}c_{gj}v_{gj}$ be correct weight.
Report conditional agreement $K_{\mathrm{corr}}/M_{\mathrm{cov}}$ when $M_{\mathrm{cov}}>0$, weighted coverage $M_{\mathrm{cov}}$, raw completion counts, and the missing-outcome interval
\begin{equation}
 [K_{\mathrm{corr}},\ K_{\mathrm{corr}}+1-M_{\mathrm{cov}}].
 \label{eq:missing}
\end{equation}
For $n$ equally weighted pairs with $m$ complete and $k$ correct, this reduces to $[k/n,(k+n-m)/n]$.
The interval bounds full-cohort agreement under arbitrary missing outcomes; it is not a statistical confidence interval.
A missing repeat is not replaced by the median of surviving calls.
Completeness includes selected routes and tools, not only a final score.
A valid observation that an artifact fails a quality check differs from an execution error.

\paragraph{Ranking and selection endpoints.}
For the default $N=4$ design, human complete rankings support all strict pairs.
Global agreement averages within input groups before aggregation across inputs.
Top-region agreement uses prespecified strict comparisons involving at least one human top-two output.
Best-of-$N$ measures selection of a human top-ranked output; normalized rank regret is defined in Section~\ref{sec:core}.
Human ties, evaluator ties, and incomplete rankings are governed by a frozen policy and reported separately.
Pair-only data do not supply missing ranks or cardinal utility.
Core $H_1$ agreement is reported by dimension before any macro average.
Groups without a strict human preference are ineligible for strict agreement and retain a separate count; they are not assigned artificial pair labels.
Repeated appearances of an artifact use its frozen scores, so pairs sharing an input or artifact are not independent observations.

\paragraph{Randomness and repeated use.}
Freeze $R\geq3$, score precision, retry policy, artifact reuse, and cache identity.
The target in Equation~\ref{eq:objective} averages over this fixed execution protocol as well as the input distribution; a finite set of recorded executions is one realization, not the population median.
Retries are outcome-blind and uniformly applied.
Repeated scores are not independent task observations.
Paired uncertainty uses original-input/source group resampling, preserving output groups and comparisons; the planned interval level is 95\%.
Report independent search seeds separately from scoring repeats, and distinguish within-run execution variation from between-search variation.
Retain failed, rejected, incomplete, and interrupted attempts in the authorized analysis, without favorable resampling or denominator changes.

\paragraph{C2/C3 human outcomes.}
Report wins, losses, ties, invalid outputs, and major regressions per input group.
A prespecified tie-aware preference probability may assign half credit to a tie,
with its denominator and missingness bounds stated. Report C2 changes against
the shared $y_0$ in addition to the critic contrast, and C3 changes against Base
in addition to the reward contrast. Intervals and costs accompany effect sizes.
Multiple domains, dimensions, and ablations require declared primary contrasts
and a prespecified multiplicity policy. A search-selected development maximum
is not substituted for any final endpoint.

\paragraph{Common human feedback and selection.}
All arms use the same proposal-Train inputs, artifacts, and available human feedback levels.
A fine-tuned evaluator learns pointwise scores from the permitted comparisons, with dimensional feedback when available; missing human explanations are not replaced by extra teacher labels in this contrast.
Scoring remains pointwise and label-blind.
Sealed Train supplies aggregate measurements only, never per-example training targets or gradients.

Human units, annotation time, unsuccessful trials, optimization compute, tool calls, and selection costs are all counted.
Adaptation and deployment costs are reported separately, with matched-budget comparisons wherever feasible and unmatched resources explicitly identified.

\paragraph{Separating observation from interpretation.}
The end-to-end comparison permits IterEval to change its observation path. It cannot
by itself attribute a difference to better interpretation of identical evidence.
A shared-observation control therefore freezes $(A,T,\Pi)$ and supplies the same
artifact evidence to fine-tuning and IterEval restricted to $(K,V,G)$. This control,
together with the component comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:full-ablations}, tests
whether changed observations account for any advantage. Neither lower annotation
cost nor superiority to fine-tuning is assumed. A combined weight-and-program
arm would be needed to test complementarity.

\paragraph{Inspectable does not mean faithful.}
An explicit rubric and evidence trace make the stated basis of a judgment
available for inspection. They do not establish that the judgment follows that
basis or matches the human standard. Mechanism assessment must check cited
observations and test predicted decision changes under controlled evidence or
component interventions. These tests are distinct from preference agreement.

The tuning comparison concerns the \emph{evaluator}. C3 instead trains a
\emph{generator} against frozen evaluator rewards. Neither experiment has been
executed in this draft. The simulated C1 displays illustrate a subset of reporting
conditions and contain no fine-tuning or calibration outcomes.

\section{Trajectory, mechanism, and efficiency analysis}
\label{app:analysis-details}

\paragraph{From unsuccessful implementation to accepted change.}
For each selected trajectory, report the direction's initial error, intended correction, candidate sequence, diagnostic measurements, acceptance decisions, and next working programs.
A favorable example must show what changed between the failed and successful attempts, not merely that the later score is higher.
Include unsuccessful and exhausted directions under the same selection rule.
Report the agent's reason for ending the direction alongside the available diagnostic results, distinguishing implementation problems from an implemented correction that did not address the target errors.
That decision itself is not evidence that the direction is unhelpful.

\paragraph{Controlled component attribution.}
Compare compatible tool, route, rubric, module, and synthesis variants on the same inputs.
Hold upstream observations fixed when testing their interpretation; rerun the full program when the observation path changes.
A tool can matter only if its output reaches a consumer and changes the resulting judgment.
Interactions between components prevent interpreting full-minus-fixed effects as additive contributions.
Only after these tests should a failure be assigned to missing evidence, incorrect interpretation, or insufficient feedback.

\paragraph{Feedback and adapter fidelity.}
Assess $H_0$ through $H_3$ cumulatively on the same pairs and include annotation time.
Direct and converted preferences are compared on the same original-input subset, first for label fidelity and then for downstream agreement.
A label conversion does not create missing human rationales or dimensional judgments.
Agreement among existing signals and agreement among annotators are distinct measurements.

\paragraph{Efficiency and deployment.}
Report data-mining computation, human time, search cost, and deployment cost separately.
Search cost includes rejected candidates, diagnostic calls, retries, and terminal assessment.
Repeated pointwise scoring measures execution variation; independent search seeds measure optimizer variation.
A cost--agreement profile is not an evolution trajectory, and a longer direction does not by itself establish efficient search.

\paragraph{Annotation outcomes.}
The annotation fixture assigns three simulated raters per group.
Preferences for the two outputs are encoded as 0/1, a tie as 2, and abstention as $-1$.
Agreement is computed within each group from all available non-abstaining rater pairs, then averaged across eligible groups.
Fewer than two valid votes is insufficient; three distinct valid votes yields no consensus.
A two-of-three majority gives a decisive preference or a tie.
These outcomes are not evidence about actual annotators or any public benchmark.

\paragraph{Reproduction and replacement.}
The offline script \path{scripts/render_simulated_results.py}, seeded with
20260916, produces the CSV fixtures, vector figures, numerical table, and a
manifest of hashes. Every data row carries a \texttt{SIMULATED} provenance
field, and every chart discloses its status within the image. The committed
assets compile without model access. Before a results-bearing submission,
replace the fixtures with authorized measurements, regenerate captions and
uncertainty, and audit all claims against the actual evidence. Removing a
disclosure without replacing its data is not a valid conversion to results.
