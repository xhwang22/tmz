\section{Domains, data sources, and study readiness}
\label{app:domains}

A \emph{domain} is a collection of tasks sharing core quality dimensions, their priorities, the overall decision rule, and the human annotation protocol.
The experimental unit is a separately adapted evaluator within one such standard.
Table~\ref{tab:domains} lists 22 candidate domains in four families; inclusion does not establish experimental readiness.
C1 assesses every admitted domain in this scope; C2 and C3 use the common representative roster TD-SV4, TD-IG1, TD-TG1, and TD-AR1.
Public resource availability determines candidate routes, independently of the local execution inventory.
A domain enters the final comparison only after its data, standard, executable interface, and independent test are frozen.
All admitted domains must be reported regardless of their results.

For each admitted domain, the execution record fixes the source version, quality standard, output-generator mixture, outputs per input, and direct, converted, or newly annotated label route.
Record original-input and larger source-group counts separately, together with pair counts and the number of fully human-ranked groups eligible for selection.

WebDev and WebGen are distinct cohorts: record their roles and audit cross-source overlap instead of treating their rows as interchangeable.
The common roster fixes coverage, not a requirement for favorable outcomes; failed preflight, incomplete execution, and null effects retain their domain labels.

These fields are frozen before running the study.

\paragraph{Within-domain assessment.}
Val and Test are reserved from the domain source pool before mining, under the same fixed quality standard as Train.
This is an independent within-domain test, not a claim that arbitrary distribution shifts are solved.
Held-out selection uses neither mining scores nor evaluator outcomes.
Source inclusion rules, generator mixture, and duplicate thresholds are frozen before splitting.
All derivatives of the same document, website/template, image cluster, or research topic remain together at the declared dependence level across Train, Val, and Test.
An overlap audit checks source groups and derived artifacts across all three partitions.
An optional source or generator shift must name what changes and verify that the human standard remains comparable.
Harder cases alone do not define a distinct domain.

\section{Human feedback and preference mining}
\label{app:annotation}

Human feedback has four levels: $H_0$ gives overall preferences; $H_1$ gives dimensional preferences; $H_2$ identifies error type, severity, and location; $H_3$ optionally supplies a free-form rationale.
An $H_0$-only corpus remains usable, but it supplies neither human dimensional judgments nor human explanations.
New labels are not assumed to be more reliable than reused labels.
Both feedback routes provide the same interface to evolution while preserving the source of each label (Table~\ref{tab:human-provenance}).
A record can be written as
\begin{equation}
 h=(x,y_a,y_b,q,p,a,\nu),
 \label{eq:human}
\end{equation}
where $q$ is overall quality or a dimension, $p$ is a preference, tie, or abstention, $a$ records annotator agreement, and $\nu$ records provenance.

\paragraph{Label-blind selection.}
Deduplicate original-input groups and reserve Val and Test before mining, following Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}.
Within Train, freeze signal definitions and source mappings, select the random anchor without labels, and use only authorized anchor feedback for any human reliability qualification.
Freeze the targeted sampling policy before revealing the remaining selected labels.
For existing datasets, replay exactly this label-access order.
The random sampling arm includes its anchor within its uniform budget, not as extra data.
Both routes retain the unselected Train pool for accounting; the miner has no access to Val or Test.
Human qualification is not inferred from agreement with a model-annotated pilot.

\paragraph{Implemented conflict ranking.}
Preference mining computes structural disagreement without reading the pair's human preference.
Outputs are grouped by their original task input and deduplicated before comparison.
For a pair $r=(x,y_a,y_b)$, signal $j$ supplies a preference sign $u_j(r)\in\{-1,0,+1,\bot\}$ from its own score difference and declared tie margin.
Zero denotes a tie and $\bot$ missing or abstaining output; raw scores from different signals are never subtracted.
Signals require executable sources with compatible inputs; provenance, repeated-run variation, and missingness are recorded separately.
We freeze a many-to-many mapping $M_{jq}$ from signal $j$ to dimension $q$ using its definition and measurement procedure before reading preference labels.
A mixed score may connect to several dimensions, but remains one shared signal.
Signals sharing a model, training labels, or underlying metric are assigned source groups so that duplicated prompts do not become independent votes.
Metrics reserved for final independent assessment remain outside mining, evaluator tools, critique, and reward.

The ranking distinguishes three cases.
\emph{Within-dimension disagreement} occurs when signals from distinct source groups favor opposite outputs on the same criterion.
\emph{Overall--dimension disagreement} occurs when an overall signal opposes a dimensional signal.
\emph{Cross-dimensional trade-offs} occur when dimension-level preferences favor different outputs; these are not contradictory measurements of one criterion.
Their indicators give the structural priority in Equation~\ref{eq:conflict}.
Pairs are ranked by decreasing $P(r)$, with stable pair identifiers breaking ties.
The coefficients define a structural ranking, not estimated signal reliability or a probability of evaluation error.
In particular, $P(r)=0$ can reflect missing signals or ties rather than agreement, and agreement itself need not be correct.
Overall--dimension comparisons retain whether their signals share a source group; they are not automatically independent evidence.
Each source group contributes at most one preference sign to a dimension's aggregate preference, with opposing within-group signs yielding abstention.
The dimension's preference is the sign of the sum of these source-group votes, with a zero sum yielding no preference.
Missing outputs and small-margin ties remain distinct from opposing preferences.

For repeated model signals, the pairwise signal margin is the median of same-repeat score differences between the two artifacts; variation and sign changes are retained.
This differs from Equation~\ref{eq:measurement}, which first takes each artifact's median final score and then compares them.
A missing required signal repeat makes that evaluation signal unavailable for mining.
Deterministic metrics use declared ranges and orientations, not ranges fitted to the output pool or human labels.
The random anchor is selected by original-input hashes before scoring, preserving output groups.
Label-blind execution does not establish label-blind upstream cohort selection; historical data must also document how the output pool was chosen.

\paragraph{Frozen mining configuration.}
For each domain, record the executable signal roster and its size, source groups, dimension mapping, score orientation, tie margins, repeat count, and random-anchor input identifiers before revealing preferences.
The native pool runner defaults to three repeats and a two-point tie margin for model scores; metric bundles supply their own normalized tie margins.
An available margin whose absolute value does not exceed its signal's threshold is a tie.
Anchor size belongs to the pool contract, not to the conflict formula, and the targeted queue uses rank order rather than a softmax sampling distribution.
The current draft does not freeze a cross-domain human-study anchor fraction or signal roster; these remain execution settings to fill in Table~\ref{tab:manifest}.
Pilot anchor defaults must not be reported as human-study settings.

\paragraph{Human qualification and implementation boundary.}
The implemented miner supplies the structural queue and its source-dependence records.
The full preference-mining protocol additionally requires human-annotated anchors to qualify signals before targeted annotation; cross-domain human-reliability calibration and the equal-budget advantage remain unverified.
Model-annotated pilot anchors measure agreement with that model, not human reliability.
The annotation fraction, qualification thresholds, and targeted policy must be frozen for the human study, rather than inherited silently from a pilot.
The feedback table $Z$ is a logical join over feedback, artifact, signal, and execution records; absent human dimensions or rationales remain absent.


\paragraph{Source readiness.}
A signal is marked executable only after its input contract, output orientation, dependencies, and failure behavior are verified.
A publication, URL, or repeated prompt does not establish executable or independent evidence.
Report missing dimensions and correlated source groups rather than creating artificial votes.
External metrics reserved for assessment must not enter mining or the evolved evaluator.

\paragraph{Sampling and frozen cohorts.}
The source pool, output-generation mixture, and pair eligibility rules are fixed before mining or optimizer access.
A fixed-seed random split assigns original-input groups to Train, Val, and Test, keeping any larger declared dependence groups intact.
The input pool is frozen at this split; subsequent output generation for mining uses only the assigned Train inputs.
Val and Test inputs and outputs receive no additions or replacements, and new inputs enter a later frozen dataset version.
The slide reference allocation reserves 100 Val pairs and 400 Test pairs, drawing one eligible pair uniformly within each held-out original-input group.
Group assignment and pair selection use neither human preferences, mining scores, nor evaluator outcomes.
This group-first sampling defines the evaluation population within the source pool; strict-preference agreement conditions on pairs with a strict human judgment.
Human ties and abstentions are reported separately, with the scored cohort and denominator frozen before search.
Disagreement mining and random-anchor selection are confined to the remaining Train pool.
Val and Test are held fixed, and all compared methods in a run share the same split and held-out pairs.
The run manifest records the source-pool version and hash, grouping and inclusion rules, split seed, selected pair identifiers, and cohort hashes.
Outputs generated for the prespecified refinement and reward-guided training assessments are separate evaluation products, not additions to the mining or checkpoint-selection cohorts.

Val labels and traces remain controller-only, even when a checkpoint is rejected.
Within an active direction, Val does not change the anchor, working evaluator, permitted modification scope, or direction-ending decision.
Published optimizers may retain their native development rules, but must declare their Train/Val access and receive no Test feedback.
Interrupted runs retain their failures and incurred costs; they do not receive a fresh selection opportunity.

\paragraph{Controller accuracy versus reporting weights.}
Train comparisons use equal-pair correct-ordering frequencies on a fixed cohort, as in Equation~\ref{eq:paired-gain}.
Each complete replicate contributes one only when the evaluator assigns a higher score to the human-preferred output; one replicate reduces to binary correctness.
Ties count as errors, and missing executions are not repairs.
The group-weighted endpoint below is retained for frozen Val/Test reporting; it uses one ordering after the declared within-output aggregation, not the average of arbitrarily many repeated orderings.
Record both the case weights and aggregation rule rather than treating these estimators as interchangeable.

\paragraph{Agreement estimator.}
For Val/Test reporting, freeze the deployed evaluator's scoring protocol, including the number $K$ of executions per artifact, aggregation, precision, retries, and cache identity.
The existing reporting plan uses $K\geq3$ and median aggregation; this is not a requirement to repeat every Train candidate measurement three times.
Train uses the separately frozen initial allocation and decision-focused repetition policy in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm}.
Let $P_g$ be the strict human preferences within original-input group $g$.
For complete executions,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bar s_\theta(x,y)&=\median_{r=1,\ldots,K}s_\theta^{(r)}(x,y),\\
 v_{gj}&=\ind[\bar s_\theta(x_g,y_{gj}^{+})>\bar s_\theta(x_g,y_{gj}^{-})],\\
 \widehat J(\theta)&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal G|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}
       \frac{1}{|P_g|}\sum_{j\in P_g}v_{gj}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
Input groups receive equal weight; predicted ties count as incorrect.
Human ties do not create strict preference labels.
Groups without a strict preference retain a separate count and do not enter this endpoint.
The reference allocation uses one pair per held-out original input; domains with multiple scored pairs per group use the group-weighted estimator above.
A reused artifact keeps its frozen repeated scores across all comparisons.

\paragraph{Missing execution and coverage.}
Fix weights $w_{gj}=1/(|\mathcal G||P_g|)$ before execution.
Let $c_{gj}$ indicate complete scoring of both artifacts, $M_{\mathrm{cov}}=\sum w_{gj}c_{gj}$, and $K_{\mathrm{corr}}=\sum w_{gj}c_{gj}v_{gj}$.
Report conditional agreement $K_{\mathrm{corr}}/M_{\mathrm{cov}}$, weighted coverage, raw counts, and
\begin{equation}
 [K_{\mathrm{corr}},\,K_{\mathrm{corr}}+1-M_{\mathrm{cov}}],
 \label{eq:missing}
\end{equation}
the possible full-cohort agreement range under arbitrary missing outcomes.
This is not a confidence interval.
Main agreement contrasts use a common complete cohort across the compared methods, with the requested-cohort bounds alongside them; method-specific complete cohorts are supplementary.
Missing required repeats or tool results remain missing, rather than being replaced by successful retries selected after seeing their score.
Valid evidence of a low-quality artifact is an outcome, not an execution failure.

\paragraph{Selection and top-region judgments.}
Freeze a common output pool size for the main selection cohort; the initial design proposes $N=4$, subject to data readiness.
Other $N$ values are separate sensitivity analyses.
Complete human rankings, including declared ties, identify the set of top-ranked outputs.
Best-of-$N$ succeeds when the evaluator selects a member of that set.
Resolve evaluator score ties by a fixed output-identifier rule independent of human rank.
With human ties, define regret as the number of outputs strictly preferred to the selected output divided by $N-1$; for strict rankings this is $(\operatorname{rank}(\hat y)-1)/(N-1)$ for the selected output $\hat y$.
For a human-ranked group without a valid evaluator selection, count selection as unsuccessful and regret as one, and additionally report complete-case sensitivity.
Top-region agreement uses strict pairs touching the human top-two region, including any ties at its boundary.
No-consensus or insufficient human rankings are reported separately and never supplied by the evaluator.
Pair-only data cannot establish any of these multi-output endpoints.

\paragraph{Aggregation and uncertainty.}
Freeze the admitted domain set per endpoint before outcomes are opened and use equal-domain means.
Report the domain count, input/source groups, pairs, outputs, ranking pool size, and completion for every number.
Separate reused-label and new-label results before combining compatible endpoints.
Do not pool incomparable quality metrics or replace an unavailable domain after observing its performance.

The main performance estimate averages independently completed searches, not the best search selected retrospectively.
Static methods have no search-seed variance.
The initial target is five independent searches per adaptive condition, with the final count and sample sizes frozen after a development-only feasibility and variance pilot.
Report all run estimates when the available count is too small for stable intervals.
For fixed evaluators, compute paired 95\% bootstrap intervals by resampling original-input/source groups jointly across methods, retaining all their outputs.
For optimizer comparisons, additionally resample independent search runs, paired only when the experimental blocks were deliberately paired; otherwise resample method runs independently.
The same input resample is used across methods and runs.
Scoring repeats do not increase the number of independent tasks or searches.
If shared reviewers introduce additional dependence, retain reviewer identifiers and report reviewer-cluster or crossed-cluster sensitivity rather than treating votes as independent.

Predeclare one principal evaluator comparison (EvalOpt versus GEPA), the Seed contrast, and the direction-versus-history contrast.
Report paired effect sizes and intervals for all other comparisons, with a prespecified multiplicity correction for any family of significance claims.
Select sample sizes from a target minimum effect and development-only variance, not from a desired test outcome.
A nonsignificant difference does not establish equivalence.
Bootstrap intervals on one final evaluator do not establish search stability.

\paragraph{Independent checkpoints.}
The primary endpoint evaluates the terminal Val-selected evaluator after search and all configuration choices are frozen.
A prespecified secondary analysis records the best evaluator $B$ at 25, 50, 75, and 100\% of the search cost cap, using the last completed promotion before each boundary.
These checkpoints are scored in the same post-freeze Test assessment phase, without returning their outcomes to search or selecting a different terminal evaluator.
A run ending early carries its last best evaluator forward, with its stopping reason retained.
Scoring costs for the additional checkpoints are recorded separately and cannot purchase more search.
The terminal comparison remains the primary estimate; intermediate Test points describe the frozen search trajectory rather than repeated opportunities for selection.

\section{Depth-first evaluator evolution: control and measurement}
\label{app:algorithm}

Execution acceptance, Train direction control, and Val promotion have different inputs and state effects.
This section specifies the reference controller, \texttt{bounded\_direction\_v2}, and its measurement rules.
These rules define an empirical search policy; their implementation does not establish improved evaluator accuracy.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:era} gives the loop, and Appendix~\ref{app:synthesis} describes the concrete evaluator used by the reference implementation.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Direction development with confirmed anchors and bounded exploration}
\label{alg:era}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Seed $E_0$, Train, sealed Val, frozen measurement and control settings
\State Measure $E_0$ on Val; set $B\gets E_0$; initialize history
\While{search resources remain}
  \State Propose direction $d$; initialize $R=W=B$, scope, and direction limits
  \State Freeze direction panel $\mathcal D_d$ from the selected Train cases
  \While{$d$ is active and its resources remain}
    \State Save parent $P\gets W$; submit candidate $C$ and pre-execution predictions
    \State Validate the edit and measure $P,C,R$ under matched Train conditions
    \If{required executions are incomplete}
      \State Record the interruption; \Return the last valid $B$
    \EndIf
    \State Record effective changes, declared-check outcomes, gains, and protected losses
    \If{$C$ qualifies for the single confirmation batch}
      \State Remeasure all compared evaluators on the same Train cases under its frozen scope
      \State Use the new batch, including check outcomes, for the decision
    \EndIf
    \State Confirm $R=W=C$, stage $W=C$, or roll back $W=R$
    \State Apply the decision rules below; update pending age, rejection count, and scope
    \State Retain observations and interpretations separately, including unsuccessful attempts
    \If{a checkpoint is due and $W$ is execution-valid}
      \State Compare $W,B$ on full Val; apply Equation~\ref{eq:acceptance}
      \State Release only promotion and retained score; leave Train states unchanged
    \EndIf
    \State End $d$ on the evolver's recorded decision or a direction limit
  \EndWhile
  \State Perform the scheduled exit checkpoint; archive the direction before reset
\EndWhile
\State Complete any outstanding final checkpoint; freeze and return $B$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evaluator roles and direction history.}
The anchor $R$ is the direction's initial reference; each subsequent replacement requires sufficient Train support.
The working evaluator $W$ is the executable parent of the next revision and may temporarily lack confirmed benefit.
The best evaluator $B$ is retained by the frozen Val protocol.
At direction initialization $R=W=B$; subsequent Val promotions change only $B$.
Confirming $W$ on Train updates $R$, whereas staging changes $W$ without changing $R$.
Rollback restores the exact anchor, including configuration, prompts, routing, and dependencies.
Diagnostic history is not rolled back.
The random anchor used by preference mining is a sample of comparisons, not the anchor evaluator.

A direction records the target failure, intended correction, and accumulated attempts.
The evolver uses failed attempts to revise the correction's implementation or choose another diagnostic.
It distinguishes a missing observation, an unused observation, and an observation that changed the decision without improving preference agreement.
Replacing the intended correction requires a new direction; an implementation repair can continue the current one after rollback.
This semantic distinction is maintained by the evolver, while the controller enforces measurable validity, staging, and resource limits.

\paragraph{Pre-execution predictions.}
The revision identifies its changed components and intended effect.
Observable intermediate checks and numerical correctness predictions are optional.
Type and predictions are not reassigned after results arrive.
A numerical gain prediction is optional and must name its metric, reference evaluator, cohort, and units.
For an explicit scaffolding step, the record additionally specifies the next integration step and an intermediate success condition.
Such a step can produce evidence before a later revision connects it to the scoring decision, but it receives no exemption from the bounded exploration window.

A declared intermediate check identifies a case, output field, and comparison predicate.
Eligible structured predictions must match on every diagnostic repeat, with at least one eligible output changing or its node being created on every repeat.
Protected outputs may remain unchanged.
Checks confined to reason text or final scores, purely semantic checks, and all-preservation declarations remain unresolved rather than demonstrating a repair.
No declared checks yields \emph{not declared}, not an automatic veto on measured gain.
Failed checks prevent both confirmation and staging; unresolved checks prevent confirmation.
An explicit scaffold requires passed intermediate checks to remain provisional.
An effectively inert scaffold can enter provisional exploration only after a valid preflight.
These checks establish observable changes, not their factual correctness or causal sufficiency.

\paragraph{Execution, behavior, and preference are different observations.}
Execution checks cover effective configuration hashes, invoked nodes, tool/model identities, input/output schemas, completion status, and evidence artifacts.
A missing invocation is an execution failure even if aggregate accuracy improves.
A successful invocation with an unfulfilled intermediate prediction is a behavioral discrepancy, not an execution failure.
A fulfilled intermediate prediction with no supported ranking gain is incomplete evidence of benefit.
Configuration changes alone cannot establish execution, and score changes alone cannot establish the intended mechanism.
Automatic field comparisons and evolver interpretations are recorded separately; ambiguous semantic checks remain unresolved.

\paragraph{Paired measurements and program identity.}
The immediate parent $P=W$ and candidate $C$ use the same cases, scoring protocol, resource allowances, and unaffected inputs.
The comparison with $R$ uses the fixed direction panel within that roster; the local comparison uses the full roster.
A cross-component edit is assessed as a bundle unless a valid separate intervention is available.
Compatible upstream evidence is reused to compare changes downstream; the resulting gain is conditional on that evidence.
The reference runtime's exact reuse and fresh-execution boundaries are given in Appendix~\ref{app:synthesis}.
Anchor confirmation under these boundaries is not a claim that all upstream stages were independently regenerated.

A measurement record identifies the entire executable program, effective configuration, component and dependency versions, input and material hashes, model/tool versions, decoding settings, scoring and aggregation rules, budget, and replicate identity.
A cache hit is valid only when these identities match the requested measurement.
Replaying a cached stochastic outcome is not a new replicate.
Effective execution identity also prevents an unchanged program from obtaining a favorable new draw merely because it was resubmitted.

Failed or missing executions retain their identities and costs.
They are not zeros, successful abstentions, or grounds for silently dropping cases.
An incomplete paired comparison cannot confirm an anchor.
The reference runtime interrupts search on incomplete seed or candidate measurements; it does not implement an automatic evidence-recovery policy.

\paragraph{A stable direction panel.}
The first at most eight selected Train IDs form the fixed direction panel $\mathcal D_d$.
This rule preserves reference cases across the direction, but does not guarantee a balanced target/counterexample composition.
The step schedule also draws local targets, declared counterexamples, and fresh Train coverage.
The default local allocation is 28 cases, with up to four target anchors and eight counterexamples, followed by fresh coverage.
Panel cases are added if absent, so the measured cohort can exceed 28 cases.
Compare $C$ with $P$ on the local cohort and with $R$ on $\mathcal D_d$, retaining panel and fresh-coverage results separately.
Step-specific diagnostic cases may vary, but their scores are not concatenated into a fictitious common improvement trajectory.
Both sources remain visible learning data, and repeated use provides no independent generalization assessment.

\paragraph{Gain, protected losses, and uncertainty.}
For each case $i$ and evaluator $E$, let $o_{i,r}(E)$ be one if repeat $r$ ranks the human-preferred output strictly higher, and zero for a wrong ordering or tie.
Unavailable executions have no correctness value.
With $m$ complete repeats, $\widehat p_i(E)$ is their mean.
The implementation uses the sum $G_{\mathcal C}=|\mathcal C|\widehat g_{\mathcal C}$, equivalent to Equation~\ref{eq:paired-gain} after rescaling.
Its empirical uncertainty buffer for comparing $E$ and $F$ is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{SE}_{\mathcal C}(E,F)
 =\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal C}
   \bigl(s_i^2(E)+s_i^2(F)\bigr)\right]^{1/2},
 \label{eq:repeat-buffer}
\end{equation}
where $s_i^2$ is the within-case sample variance across repeats.
The gain is paired by case, not by arbitrarily aligned stochastic repeat slots.
Independent permutations of repeat order therefore leave every comparison unchanged.

The one-standard-error buffer is a decision heuristic, not a confidence interval or significance test.
Finite identical observations yield zero empirical variance, not proof that true execution noise is absent.
Shared upstream evidence and correlated model calls limit the independent-arm approximation.
These Train comparisons do not estimate generalization to unseen inputs.
Protected cases are checked individually: a negative change in any protected case's mean correctness prevents confirmation.
Margins, intermediate outputs, ranking flips, score variation, calls, latency, and failures remain separate diagnostics.
Neither a wider score gap nor better evidence coverage is sufficient for anchor confirmation.

\paragraph{One bounded confirmation batch.}
The default diagnostic allocation is two repeats per evaluator and case.
A positive anchor gain that does not exceed its uncertainty buffer can qualify for one additional batch if the candidate is effective and complete, neither local mean nor protected-case means regress, and declared checks are neither failed nor unresolved.
Eligibility, all compared evaluators, cases, and repeat counts are frozen before dispatch.
The parent, candidate, and anchor receive symmetric measurements with newly executed judgment stages and fixed upstream task analysis.
The batch uses the same repeat count and Train cases as the diagnostic measurement.
Its outcomes, including intermediate checks, govern the decision even when less favorable.
They are not selectively pooled with the original observations.
Effective confirmation identities are reused across rounds, preventing repeated requests until a favorable batch appears.
This batch tests judgment variation conditional on task analysis, not fresh-input coverage or analysis stability.

\paragraph{Train decision rules.}
\label{app:direction-decisions}
The following rules apply in order after completeness and effective-change checks.
A failed declared mechanism check rejects the candidate without staging it.
Except for a preflight-validated scaffold, an ineffective edit is also rejected.

First, supported harm relative to either $P$ or $R$ triggers rollback to $R$ and narrows the scope by one level.
Harm means at least two repeats with $G<-\operatorname{SE}$, or the stop-loss condition $G+\operatorname{SE}\leq-2$ correct pairs.
Second, confirmation requires at least two repeats, panel gain $G_{\mathcal D_d}(C,R)>\operatorname{SE}_{\mathcal D_d}(C,R)$, no protected-case regression in either comparison, nonnegative local mean gain, and no failed or unresolved declared check.
Confirmation sets $R=W=C$.
An absent check does not veto this measured improvement, but is not recorded as a passed mechanism prediction.

Otherwise, a valid unresolved ordinary edit can remain provisional; an effective scaffold additionally needs passed intermediate checks.
Staging sets $W=C$ without moving $R$.
The exploratory chain permits two charged provisional steps.
A third unresolved step restores $W=R$ and records expiration, rather than restarting the window.
Confirmation or rollback resets pending age.
Unexecuted generation failures retain $W$ and pending age, subject to the separate failure limit.
Comparing every candidate against $R$ as well as $P$ prevents accumulated losses from disappearing as the parent changes.

A charged rejection restores $W=R$ and clears the provisional window; an uncharged generation retry retains the previous $W$ and pending age.
Three consecutive charged rejections end the direction; confirmation or provisional continuation resets that rejection count.
The default direction limit is 18 charged steps.
The evolver can also end a direction with a recorded reason.
These bounds constrain the duration of exploration, not the correctness of its semantic interpretation.
Rejection does not itself prescribe a new direction, and a Val non-promotion does not enter these rules.

\paragraph{Trust-region-inspired scope control.}
The permitted scope has three levels: one component, several components within a functional group, or coordinated changes across groups.
In the reference evaluator these groups are task analysis, judgment, and synthesis (Appendix~\ref{app:synthesis}).
The initial level is two.
Two consecutive supported anchor updates widen the scope by one level, up to three; supported harm narrows it by one level, down to one.
Changed-character counts remain patch resource limits, not a weighted graph distance or semantic neighborhood.
The fixed character guard is 24,000 characters.
Execution permissions and interface checks remain separate hard constraints.
High empirical noise can leave an edit unresolved without mechanically shortening its prompt.

Classical trust-region methods adapt a neighborhood using agreement between predicted and measured improvements~\citep{conn2000trust}.
Stochastic variants require additional local-model and estimate conditions~\citep{bandeira2014probabilistic,chen2018storm,blanchet2019trust}.
The categorical scope levels, evolver predictions, and adaptive Train panels here have not been shown to satisfy those conditions.
The claim is controlled exploration around a confirmed anchor, not convergence or automatic verification of the correction hypothesis.

\paragraph{An auxiliary observed-to-predicted ratio.}
A pre-execution numerical prediction may specify expected correctness probabilities on authorized Train cases.
Unspecified cases predict no change; ordinal winner expectations do not imply numerical gains.
For a positive predicted gain on the same metric, immediate parent, and cohort, the diagnostic ratio is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_t =
 \frac{\widehat g_{\mathcal C_t}(C_t,P_t)}
      {g_t^{\mathrm{pred}}}.
 \label{eq:auxiliary-ratio}
\end{equation}
The implementation requires predicted gain greater than $10^{-6}$ in the sum-gain units and complete repeated measurements.
Otherwise it records the ratio as unavailable.
This repeat gate does not certify statistical precision.
The ratio never controls confirmation, staging, rollback, or scope.
A structural step without a numerical gain prediction has no ratio; it still must pass its mechanism and staging checks.

\paragraph{Val promotion and terminal assessment.}
Eligible working evaluators are assessed at a frozen checkpoint cadence, not whenever a favorable partial Val result appears.
The default cadence is every three charged steps, with direction-exit and final checkpoints.
Both $W$ and $B$ are compared on full Val under the same frozen protocol.
Compatible cached measurements may be reused under the identity and replicate rules above.
On a complete comparison with no reduction in coverage, the promotion rule is
\begin{equation}
 B\leftarrow
 \begin{cases}
 W,&\widehat J_{\mathrm{Val}}(W)-\widehat J_{\mathrm{Val}}(B)\geq\tau_{\mathrm{Val}},\\
 B,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:acceptance}
\end{equation}
with $\tau_{\mathrm{Val}}>0$ fixed before search.
The same full cohort and failure policy apply to both evaluators.
Promotion releases only its indicator and retained score; Val cases and traces remain sealed.
No Val outcome changes $R$, $W$, scope, staging windows, or direction endings.
Uncertainty summaries and worst-stratum scores are diagnostics, not additional promotion gates.
An improvement on Val is a retention decision, not mechanism attribution or confirmation of the Train hypothesis.

Restricted feedback follows the motivation of the Ladder~\citep{blum2015ladder}; this protocol does not inherit its leaderboard bound without the corresponding assumptions and release policy.
Checkpoint allowances include a reserved final comparison of the last eligible executable $W$ saved before reset.
If the run is interrupted by integrity failure or the final comparison is incomplete, retain the last valid $B$ rather than silently selecting another evaluator.
Freeze $B$ before the single terminal Test assessment, which may contain prespecified scoring repeats.
Test is invoked separately and is never loaded by the search loop.
Additional checkpoint curves, if prespecified, are assessed after search and cannot select the reported evaluator.

\paragraph{Protocol freeze and verification.}
Every run records its controller version, code/configuration identities, case allocations, scoring rules, repeats, staging and rejection limits, scope settings, resource caps, and Val cadence/margin.
Defaults above are reference settings, not evidence of optimality or statistical power.
The 100-Val/400-Test reference allocations in Appendix~\ref{app:measurement} remain configurable data-budget choices, not a precision or power claim.
Actual settings require an implementation-linked run manifest.
Historical runs retain their original controller and evaluator versions.

Constructed boundary records test decision rules, cache identity, missing-data handling, and invariance to repeat permutations.
Controlled searches must separately compare controllers from the same evaluator seed at matched cost, before attributing differences to a changed evaluator architecture.
A known-executable structural intervention can test whether implementation progress is incorrectly rejected.
Offline replay can check decision boundaries but cannot replace fresh searches: changing the controller changes later proposals and observations.
Software checks and such proposed comparisons are not empirical evidence that EvalOpt improves agreement.

\section{Reference evaluator implementation}
\label{app:synthesis}

The reference runtime represents each evaluator as an executable evaluation program with explicit task analysis ($A$), judgment ($J$), and synthesis ($S$) components.
These stages instantiate the generic revision space in Section~\ref{sec:problem}; they are not an additional optimizer contribution or a demonstrated prerequisite for depth-first evolution.
Figure~\ref{fig:runtime} illustrates their low-level elements.

\paragraph{Task analysis and judgment.}
Task analysis reads the task and permitted source material without seeing the evaluated output, its competitor, or human labels.
It identifies evaluation items and may use an ordered model workflow.
Judgment then inspects one output through LLM evaluators, tools, subagents, and routing rules.
Its structured findings retain evidence and item references for scoring and diagnosis.
Few-shot examples can be proposed from Train feedback as editable program content.
They must not encode case identifiers, label lookups, or whole-case score tables; adding an example is not itself a successful diagnostic test.

\paragraph{Deterministic synthesis and version boundaries.}
Synthesis computes scores from structured judgment outputs through declared aggregation rules; it is not a prompted synthesizer or arbitrary generated code.
The default version, A/J/S v1, uses atomic judgments and four criterion ratings, with a weighted mean for scoring.
A separately emitted holistic score is diagnostic and does not determine that mean.
The opt-in A/J/S v2 instead uses an explicit deduction ledger.
Its availability does not establish an accuracy improvement or retroactively change the evaluator used in earlier runs.

In v2, analysis items indicate whether a requirement is required or optional and clear or uncertain.
Uncertain items are not treated as required.
Judgment records resolved statuses and a ledger containing criterion, defect kind, item identifiers, severity, and evidence.
Omitted optional items and unresolved items cannot be charged as defects.
Clear required omissions or partial fulfillment, and clear contradictions, must be accounted for.
Repeated charges for the same item identifier are rejected.
These structural checks do not verify factual truth, severity calibration, or whether differently named entries describe the same defect.

For criterion $k$, deterministic synthesis subtracts the recorded severity penalties from one, clipping at zero, then forms the configured weighted mean across criteria.
The default minor, moderate, and major penalties are $0.1$, $0.3$, and $0.6$; they are heuristic settings, not learned optima.
An unresolved required item or the absence of any resolved clear item makes the score unavailable.
Optional unknowns alone need not prevent scoring.
Insufficient evidence is not a low-quality output, and the case cannot be removed from the comparison to improve apparent accuracy.
Every required evaluator report must be sufficient, even if its fusion weight is zero.
A sufficient empty ledger can yield a score of 100; an insufficient report cannot.

\paragraph{Evidence reuse.}
Editing synthesis reuses compatible analysis and judgment outputs.
Editing judgment keeps compatible task analysis fixed and invalidates dependent judgment outputs; editing analysis invalidates its dependent judgments.
The bounded fresh-judgment batch in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} reruns judgment but still holds task analysis fixed.
Thus scoring comparisons can establish changes conditional on shared evidence without establishing independent stability of the full runtime.
Complete end-to-end assessment under the frozen Val/Test protocol remains distinct from these Train diagnostics.

\section{Search, mining, and component ablations}
\label{app:ablations}

The core ablations separate diagnostic information from controller design.
The comparisons share feedback pools, seed evaluators, model versions, editable interfaces, scoring rules, Val access, and total search cost caps except for the stated intervention.
Each condition is evaluated through independent searches and its frozen terminal evaluator.
Local repair rates and individual traces are secondary descriptions of the search process.

\paragraph{Diagnostic feedback and retained history.}
The reduced-feedback condition returns paired gain, aggregate losses and uncertainty, required execution/mechanism checks, transition decisions, and cost, but omits case-level repair/regression records and intermediate traces.
Contracts, case selection, execution, and all controller checks remain enabled.
Their internal records are retained for analysis but are unavailable to the proposer through prompts, files, or diagnostic tools.
This contrast tests access to detailed diagnostic feedback, not removal of the experiment contract.

The no-retained-history condition receives the full current diagnostic record but no earlier execution records, failed-candidate archive, or summaries of those records.
Each proposer call is constructed from the permitted context, including the current program and active hypothesis, so omitted records do not remain in an inherited conversation.
The working evaluator still carries the consequences of earlier revisions, including staged changes.
Both comparisons charge all executed checks and report actual context and proposal costs; fewer prompt tokens are part of the intervention, not additional free search.
The disclosure policy is fixed before execution and audited on the actual proposer inputs.

\paragraph{Controller and modification-scope controls.}
First compare the previous and proposed controllers from the same seed, holding the evaluator architecture, editable interfaces, feedback, and total cost cap fixed.
Pin the previous controller's configuration; this is a comparison of control policies, not an isolated test of one ratio.
Within the proposed controller, compare adaptive categorical scope with a scope fixed before search, keeping confirmation, staging, and direction limits unchanged.
Character and call caps remain matched resource constraints rather than substitutes for scope.
Repeat the controller comparison with structural evolution only after the fixed-architecture comparison, using a common minimal $A/J/S$ seed and a known-executable structural intervention as a positive control.

Comparator thresholds and scope settings receive a common Train-only tuning allowance and are frozen before the independent comparisons.
Report the chosen settings and the cost of uncertainty resolution, including unsuccessful repeats.
Offline replay of fixed records can expose different confirmation, staging, or rollback decisions, but changed decisions alter subsequent proposals.
Controller efficacy therefore requires new online searches; replay is a boundary check, not a substitute.

\paragraph{Staging and direction continuation.}
The no-staging condition requires every new working evaluator to qualify for anchor confirmation, retaining diagnostics from unsuccessful candidates.
It preserves the measurement protocol, permitted scope, and execution-repair allowance.
This tests whether bounded intermediate evaluators help implement corrections whose benefit requires coordinated steps.
The immediate-branching condition ends a direction after the first execution-valid candidate that does not qualify for confirmation, retaining history and initializing the next direction with $R=W=B$.
This comparison includes the loss of staging opportunities and the new direction's panel and scope initialization; it does not isolate a semantic label alone.
Invalid submissions use the same bounded implementation-repair rules in both conditions.
An additional parent-reset condition returns to $R$ after an unsuccessful exploratory step while retaining the direction and history, isolating the choice of executable parent from direction termination.
All conditions keep Val cadence and released feedback fixed.

\paragraph{Case selection and interpretation.}
Choose trajectories using a declared rule over outcome categories and stable identifiers, not by visual appeal.
Review a random sample of all directions and separately sample any later-success, no-gain, and exhausted categories.
Where feasible, reviewers assess intended corrections and observable behavior without knowing the comparative final scores.
A representative case must identify what was missing in the earlier implementation and what changed later.
Improvement after several revisions is temporal evidence; the controlled ablations are needed to attribute a benefit to continuation.
Direction endings may follow controller thresholds, evolver decisions, or resource limits; none establishes that the correction was impossible.

\paragraph{Failure taxonomy and reporting.}
Classify observed missing evidence, unused tool outputs, interpretation errors, incompatible edits, and uncertain diagnoses.
Allow multiple causes and unknown status.
Report the share of directions with no accepted candidate, early endings, repeated equivalent edits, and tests that added no new observation.
Distinguish technical failure, lack of measured gain, and unresolved questions at the budget limit.
These analyses explain costs and limitations without turning plausible explanations into causal facts.

\paragraph{From execution evidence to subsequent revision.}
For each attempt, retain the proposed change and any pre-execution predictions, program and measurement identities, target and counterexample outcomes, execution/mechanism checks, paired gain and uncertainty, losses, stability, cost, and the resulting anchor/working transition.
Distinguish undeclared, unresolved, passed, and failed intermediate checks.
Record the permitted scope and staging window separately; store $\rho$ only when its availability conditions hold.
Report absent behavior separately from changed behavior that fails its predicted postcondition.
Within changed-output attempts, distinguish target repairs offset by regressions from gains confined to non-target cases; these descriptors can overlap and are not a forced partition.
For the subsequent attempt, record whether the evolver revises the observable implementation, its downstream use, or the correction hypothesis, allowing unknown or multiple categories.
Coding follows a prespecified rubric and separates measured behavior from the evolver's stated rationale.

The analysis includes every rejected attempt and staged candidate, including those with no subsequent revision because the direction or run ends.
Report next-attempt outcomes and remaining-budget censoring separately, along with total cost spent on directions that never promote a checkpoint.
Local follow-up rates are descriptive because the interventions can change which failures occur.
The primary comparison remains terminal Test performance across all search runs, not a success rate conditioned on eventually successful directions.
No scaffold-consumer linkage or mechanism correctness is assumed from a shared direction identifier.
