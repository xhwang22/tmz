\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Open-ended evaluation requires case-specific judgments across quality dimensions \citep{ye2024flask,kim2024evallm}.
A summary may require completeness or brevity; a slide for live discussion may prioritize clarity over detail.
Metrics and model-based evaluators do not consistently match human preferences \citep{fabbri2021summeval,xu2023imagereward,zheng2023judging,hu2024criteria}, yet humans often cannot express their judgments as an executable evaluation procedure \citep{shankar2024evalgen}.

Existing approaches use human feedback to train evaluator models \citep{xu2023imagereward}, learn or refine evaluation criteria \citep{liu2024autocalibrate,lv2026queryrubrics}, and select evaluation functions \citep{shankar2024evalgen}.
Prompt and skill optimizers offer a complementary mechanism for revising instructions from execution feedback \citep{agrawal2026gepa,yang2026skillopt}.
EvalOpt uses preferences to revise an explicit evaluation procedure---its prompts, tool use, component connections, and scoring rules---without updating the base model.
This raises two challenges: obtaining useful human preferences and turning them into improvements to the procedure.

Under a limited feedback budget, comparison selection affects learning efficiency \citep{muldrew2024activepreference,oh2025activepreference}.
Hand-designed comparisons reflect anticipated failures, while broad collections can contain easy or redundant cases.
Disagreement-based preference mining ranks comparisons by conflicting signals within the same quality dimension and retains a random anchor to expose errors shared by those signals.
Selected feedback comes from compatible existing labels or new human annotation; disagreement determines what to query, not the correct preference.

Pairwise preferences do not specify the fine-grained criteria behind a choice \citep{ye2024flask,lv2026queryrubrics}.
A useful correction may require several revisions: a proposed tool call may not execute, or its evidence may not affect scoring.
An unsuccessful candidate therefore need not invalidate the correction.
In the default evolution setup we tested, the model proposed revisions from previous candidates and outcomes, but often switched directions after unsuccessful early candidates.

Depth-first evaluator evolution maintains an improvement direction---a target failure and proposed correction---across revisions (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}, EvalOpt panel; Figure~\ref{fig:overview}, Evaluator evolution panel).
Diagnostics guide repair or reconsideration even after candidate rollback.
Continuation is separate from candidate acceptance and global retention at validation (Val) checkpoints.
Directions end on diagnostic-based decisions, rejection limits, or resource limits.

We compare EvalOpt with Seed, learned signal fusion, SkillOpt, GEPA, and Meta-Harness across 19 equally weighted domains spanning four task families (Table~\ref{tab:main-alignment}).
Held-out decisive-pair accuracy measures whether independently scored outputs match human preferences under common feedback and search budgets.
Webpage generation, image captioning, summarization, and research-idea generation support search controls, sampling comparisons, and independently judged downstream applications.

EvalOpt reaches \ResultPair\% accuracy across 19 domains, exceeding Seed by \ResultSeedGain{} percentage points and the strongest baseline, GEPA, by \ResultGepaGain{} points.
On the four analysis domains, it reaches 72.1\%, versus 68.2\% for fixed patience and 67.4\% for history-based search (Table~\ref{tab:search-results}).
EvalOpt and fixed patience average 3.8 and 3.6 revisions per direction; similar mean depths are consistent with a benefit from diagnostic-guided continuation but do not establish its cause.

On the same four domains, mined feedback raises EvalOpt accuracy from 70.3\% to 72.1\% at 100 revealed input groups, with total model-and-tool costs of 106 versus 102 units for random sampling (Table~\ref{tab:mining-results}).
Blinded judgments favor EvalOpt-guided over Seed-guided outputs at tie-aware rates of 60.8\% for critique-guided refinement and 59.1\% for reward-guided training (Figure~\ref{fig:budget-tests}b).
These downstream comparisons assess output quality independently of the optimized evaluator.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We contribute preference-guided revision of executable evaluators with fixed base-model weights, combining disagreement-based mining with depth-first evolution that retains diagnostics after rejection.
A 19-domain evaluation and four-domain controls assess agreement, search, feedback efficiency, and downstream utility.
